## 1. THE BUSINESS — Understanding the Economic Engine

### What Does Shilchar Do?

Shilchar Technologies Ltd, incorporated in 1986 and headquartered in Vadodara, Gujarat, manufactures power & distribution transformers and electronics & telecom transformers. The company specialises in customised, high-efficiency transformers for renewable energy (solar/wind), industrial, utility, and export markets. Products range from 5 KVA to 3,000 KVA distribution transformers and 3 MVA to 50 MVA / 132 kV class power transformers.

### Would Buffett Understand It?

Absolutely. This is an old-economy, essential infrastructure business. Transformers are the unglamorous plumbing of the electrical grid — every watt of electricity generated, transmitted, or distributed requires a transformer. Without them, India's power grid, renewable energy ambitions, and industrial economy cannot function.

### Business Model Type

Shilchar is best characterised as a **niche custom manufacturer with switching costs** — not a pure commodity player, and not a toll bridge.

- **Not a commodity business:** Custom-built transformers to client specifications, requiring UL/CSA certifications, quality approvals, and engineering capability. Relationships, reliability, and credibility matter more than price.
- **Switching costs exist:** Once qualified by a utility or EPC contractor, switching carries quality/delivery risk. Shilchar's 40-year track record and export certifications create meaningful friction.
- **Not a toll bridge:** Competitive industry with 300+ players in India. But within its niche (custom transformers for renewables, exports to developed markets), it commands premium pricing reflected in ~30% operating margins.

> *"We like businesses that have some kind of moat around them — a franchise that isn't easy to replicate."* — Buffett

Shilchar's moat is narrower than Coca-Cola's brand but meaningfully differentiated within its niche: engineering credibility, export certifications, customer relationships, and specialisation in renewable energy transformers.

**Revenue Split (Approximate):** ~60% renewable energy (solar/wind), ~20% exports (US, Canada, Middle East, Europe), ~20% domestic industrial/utility.

## 8. CAPEX QUALITY ANALYSIS — Growth vs. Maintenance CapEx & Owner Earnings

### Owner Earnings Computation

**Base Owner Earnings = Net Income + D&A − Maintenance CapEx**

**Maintenance CapEx justification:** For Shilchar, maintenance CapEx ≈ Depreciation. The business is asset-light (₹59 Cr fixed assets generating ₹732 Cr TTM revenue = 12x+ asset turnover). The manufacturing facilities require minimal replacement spending — transformer manufacturing is not equipment-intensive like steel or cement. D&A of ₹3 Cr/year against ₹59 Cr gross fixed assets implies an average asset life of ~20 years, and actual replacement needs are modest. Therefore, maintenance CapEx ≈ D&A, and **Owner Earnings ≈ Net Income** for this business.

| Component | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | TTM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net Income (₹ Cr) | 43 | 92 | 147 | 185 |
| Add: D&A | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 |
| Less: Maintenance CapEx (≈D&A) | (2) | (3) | (3) | (4) |
| Less: SBC | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **= Owner Earnings** | **43** | **92** | **147** | **185** |
| **Owner Earnings Per Share (₹)** | **37.6** | **80.4** | **128.5** | **161.71** |

**SBC Materiality Gate:** SBC = 0 (zero). No stock-based compensation of any kind. This is below the 5% materiality threshold, so the full SBC & Dilution Analysis is NOT required. **Base Owner Earnings is the correct DCF starting point.** GAAP NI already includes SBC expense (zero in this case) — stated clearly per template requirements.

> *"We define 'owner earnings' as reported earnings plus depreciation, depletion, amortisation less the average annual amount of capitalised expenditures that the business requires to fully maintain its long-term competitive position and its unit volume."* — Buffett (1986 Letter). For Shilchar, Owner Earnings essentially equal Net Income because D&A ≈ maintenance CapEx and SBC = 0. Reported earnings are REAL.
