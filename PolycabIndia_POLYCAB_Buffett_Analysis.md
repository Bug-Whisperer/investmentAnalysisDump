## 1. THE BUSINESS — Understanding the Economic Engine

Polycab India is India's **largest manufacturer of wires and cables**, holding a dominant 26–27% share of the organised domestic W&C market. Founded in 1964 and listed in April 2019, the company operates through three segments:

1. **Wires & Cables (W&C) — ~84% of FY25 revenue:** The core cash engine. Polycab manufactures 10,600+ SKUs spanning flexible wires, building wires, power cables, control cables, optical fibre cables, rubber cables, and specialty cables. The business serves both retail/distribution channels (household wiring, construction) and institutional/B2B channels (infrastructure, industrial, utilities). This is a **toll-bridge business** — every building, factory, solar farm, data centre, and EV charging station needs wires and cables. Polycab collects the toll.

2. **Fast Moving Electrical Goods (FMEG) — ~9% of FY25 revenue:** A high-growth flanker strategy. Polycab leverages its brand and distribution network (4,700+ authorised dealers, 2,00,000+ retail touchpoints) to sell fans, LED lighting, switches, switchgear, water heaters, solar products, and pumps. This is a **consumer franchise** play — using the trust built in W&C to sell higher-margin consumer electricals.

3. **EPC (Engineering, Procurement & Construction) — ~7% of FY25 revenue:** Turnkey power distribution, transmission, and rural electrification projects. This is a **project business** — lumpy, lower-margin, but it deepens relationships with state utilities and provides visibility into infrastructure spending trends.

Buffett: *"I try to buy stock in businesses that are so wonderful that an idiot can run them. Because sooner or later, one will."* Polycab's W&C business is about as simple as it gets — buy copper and aluminium, draw it into wire, insulate it, and sell it through a massive distribution network into non-discretionary demand. Every house needs wiring. Every factory needs cables. The product hasn't fundamentally changed in decades, and demand is tied to electrification, construction, and infrastructure — all structural growth drivers in India. This is squarely within Buffett's circle of competence.

> **CRITICAL — Screener Data Note & Why EBITDA Is Not Used:** Screener.in's "Operating Profit" is calculated BEFORE deducting Depreciation & Amortisation, making it an EBITDA-equivalent. This prompt deliberately does NOT track EBITDA as a standalone metric. Buffett (2000 Letter): *"References to EBITDA make us shudder — does management think the tooth fairy pays for capital expenditures?"* Munger: *"I think every time you see the word EBITDA, you should substitute the phrase 'bullshit earnings.'"* Instead, **EBIT** (Operating Profit minus D&A) is tracked as the true after-capital-charge operating profit. For valuation, this prompt uses **Owner Earnings** (Section 8) and **P/Owner Earnings** — never EBITDA-based multiples.

> † **Gross Profit / Gross Margin are ESTIMATES.** Screener.in's consolidated statement bundles raw material, employee, and other operating costs without isolating a clean cost-of-goods line. These figures are estimated by adding back employee and other operating expenses to the pre-D&A operating profit, cross-checked against the ~68–76% raw-material-to-revenue ratio typical of Polycab's copper/aluminium/PVC-intensive cost base and disclosed in annual reports. Treat the gross margin trend (rising from ~17% to ~24% as the mix shifts toward higher-margin retail W&C and FMEG) as directional, not precise.

> \* **FY15 share jump:** The 100% increase in FY15 diluted shares (7.04 → 14.09 Cr) reflects a pre-IPO bonus/sub-division of equity, not a dilutive capital raise. Screener retrospectively adjusts EPS for this. From FY16 onward, share count has been essentially flat (cumulative change <8% over nine years), confirming negligible ongoing dilution.

> **Note on TTM figures:** The "TTM" column covers the trailing twelve months ending Dec 2025 (Q4FY25 + Q1–Q3FY26). The TTM Revenue Growth of **29%** is measured against the *prior comparable TTM* (twelve months ending Dec 2024 = ₹21,014 Cr), i.e. ₹27,005 Cr ÷ ₹21,014 Cr − 1 = **28.5% ≈ 29%**. It is NOT ₹27,005 ÷ FY25's ₹22,408 (which would be 20.5%, an invalid overlapping-period comparison). The TTM NI growth of 43% and EPS growth of 30% are computed on the same prior-TTM basis.

This is a business that has shifted into a higher growth gear since FY22, driven by infrastructure spending (government capex on power transmission, railways, renewable energy), housing boom, and FMEG expansion.

Operating margins (pre-D&A, as reported by Screener) have expanded from 7–9% (FY14–FY17) to a stable 12–14% band (FY19 onwards). Net profit has grown faster than revenue — a hallmark of operating leverage. The **FY24 net profit growth of 40.5%** on 27.9% revenue growth confirms that earnings power is amplifying. EPS has compounded at 21.4% over 5 years and 27.7% over 10 years — outstanding for a mid-cap industrial company.

Buffett: *"The key to investing is determining the competitive advantage of any given company and, above all, the durability of that advantage."* Polycab's ability to grow revenue at 20%+ while expanding margins from 8% to 13%+ over a decade is direct evidence of a widening competitive advantage. The numbers don't lie — this is a business where growth begets better economics, not worse.

A conversion ratio near 100% (with some years above and some below) confirms that reported earnings are **backed by real cash**. This is not an accounting illusion — the cash register matches the income statement.

As this capex normalises post-FY27, FCF conversion should improve materially.

**SBC & Buybacks:** Stock-based compensation is negligible (estimated <2% of net income, ~₹38 Cr in FY25) and there is **no buyback programme** — Polycab returns capital purely via dividends. This means reported FCF is NOT inflated by SBC add-backs, and the Owner Earnings analysis in Section 8 does not require a dilution adjustment.

**Dividends:** Dividends paid rose from ₹15 Cr (FY15) to ₹532 Cr (FY25), tracking the rise in payout ratio from 8–9% to 24–26%. This reflects management's increasing confidence in sustainable cash generation while still retaining ~74% for high-return reinvestment.

Buffett: *"Owner earnings represent (a) reported earnings plus (b) depreciation, depletion, amortization, and certain other non-cash charges less (c) the average annual amount of capitalized expenditures for plant and equipment that the business requires to fully maintain its long-term competitive position and its unit volume."* The Owner Earnings calculation follows in Section 8.

## 10. COMPETITIVE POSITION & MOAT ANALYSIS

**Moat Type: Cost Advantage + Brand + Distribution Network (Wide Moat)**

**The Castle:** India's #1 wires & cables company with ~27% organised market share, 10,600+ SKUs, 27+ manufacturing plants, 4,700+ authorised dealers, and 200,000+ retail touchpoints.

**The Moat — Multi-layered:**

**1. Scale-Driven Cost Advantage:** As the largest manufacturer, Polycab enjoys: (a) superior procurement terms for copper and aluminium (the primary raw materials, comprising 60–70% of costs); (b) manufacturing efficiency from higher utilisation across a diversified plant network; (c) logistics efficiency from strategically located plants across Gujarat, Maharashtra, Uttarakhand, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Daman. Smaller competitors cannot match these unit economics.

**2. Brand & Trust Franchise:** In wires and cables — where product failure means fire risk and safety hazard — brand trust is paramount. Polycab's brand carries a safety premium that allows pricing power. Electricians and contractors (the key purchase influencers in the retail channel) default to Polycab because their reputation is on the line.

**3. Distribution Depth:** 4,700+ dealers and 200,000+ retail points create a distribution web that is extraordinarily expensive and time-consuming to replicate. This is a physical-world moat — you can't disrupt it with an app. New entrants face a chicken-and-egg problem: they can't build distribution without brand, and can't build brand without distribution.

**4. Cross-Selling Platform (FMEG):** The W&C distribution network now carries fans, lights, switches, switchgear, and solar products — generating incremental revenue with minimal incremental distribution cost. This "land and expand" strategy is a textbook moat-widening move.

**Key Competitors:**
- **KEI Industries:** #2 player, strong in institutional/B2B cables. Competent competitor but lacks Polycab's retail distribution depth and FMEG diversification.
- **R R Kabel:** Post-IPO (2023), growing aggressively but from a much smaller base. Focused on wires (not heavy cables).
- **Havells India:** Strong FMEG player competing in switches and fans but not a serious cables competitor.
- **Finolex Cables:** Legacy player but has lost market share due to management issues and slower growth.

**Is the moat enduring or eroding?** ENDURING AND WIDENING. Polycab's market share has been stable-to-increasing (26–27% of organised market). The shift from unorganised to organised (driven by BIS quality standards, GST formalisation, and safety awareness) is a structural tailwind that benefits the market leader disproportionately.

**Susceptibility to Change:** LOW. Wires and cables are a 60+ year old product category with minimal technological disruption risk. Copper wire is copper wire. The fundamental product will look the same in 20 years. The only change vectors are: (a) shift to aluminium in some applications (Polycab produces both); (b) optical fibre (Polycab already makes OFC); (c) EV-related cables (Polycab is already developing these). All change vectors are additive, not disruptive.

Buffett: *"We do not get enthused about change, with a few exceptions, as a way to make a lot of money. We're looking for the absence of change to protect ways that are already making a lot of money."* Polycab epitomises this — the business model hasn't changed in decades, and won't change in the next two decades. This is exactly the kind of boring, predictable, growing-with-the-economy franchise that Buffett loves.

### Step 3B: Buffett-Pure Reverse DCF — Implied Owner Earnings Growth Required

> **Why this matters:** Step 3 uses EPS because the market prices on PE — it tells you what the *market* needs to believe. But EPS can be misleading: a company can grow EPS by underinvesting in maintenance, financial engineering, or aggressive accounting. Owner Earnings strips all this out — it measures the TRUE economic cash the business generates for its owner after maintaining its productive capacity. Buffett (2002 Letter): *"Trumpeting EBITDA is a particularly pernicious practice. Doing so implies that depreciation is not truly an expense, given that it is a 'non-cash' charge. That's nonsense."* The same insight applies to EPS vs. Owner Earnings.

**Formula:**
```
Required Future Price         = CMP × (1 + Hurdle Rate)^n
Required Future OEPS          = Required Future Price / Terminal P/OE Multiple
Implied OEPS CAGR             = (Required Future OEPS / Current OEPS)^(1/n) - 1
```
