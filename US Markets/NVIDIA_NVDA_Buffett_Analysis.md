## 1. THE BUSINESS — Understanding the Economic Engine

NVIDIA designs and sells accelerated computing platforms centred on Graphics Processing Units (GPUs), interconnect networking, and AI software. The business model is elegantly simple at its core: NVIDIA designs the best chips and software for parallel computation, contracts out manufacturing to TSMC, and sells the finished products at extraordinary margins to data centre operators, gamers, and enterprises.

Would Buffett understand it? The product itself is complex silicon, but the economic engine is crystal clear — NVIDIA operates a **toll bridge on the AI highway.** Every company building AI infrastructure — from hyperscalers like Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Meta to sovereign nations and enterprises — must pay the NVIDIA toll. The company captures ~80-90% of the data centre GPU market. This is a textbook Buffett franchise: high switching costs (CUDA ecosystem lock-in), network effects (the largest developer ecosystem attracts the most software, which attracts the most customers), and pricing power (gross margins above 70%).

> *"The key to investing is not assessing how much an industry is going to affect society, or how much it will grow, but rather determining the competitive advantage of any given company and, above all, the durability of that advantage."* — Warren Buffett

NVIDIA's economic engine generates revenue across four platforms: Data Centre (~90% of revenue), Gaming (~7%), Professional Visualisation (~1.5%), and Automotive (~1%). The Data Centre segment is the overwhelming driver, powered by the generational shift from general-purpose computing to accelerated computing and AI. Q1 FY2027 guidance of $78 billion (up ~77% YoY) confirms the demand trajectory remains extraordinary.

**Owner Earnings Calculation — Two Frameworks:**

*Standard Owner Earnings (accounting-correct):*
```
Net Income (FY2026):          $120,067M
+ Depreciation & Amortisation:  $5,200M
- Maintenance CapEx (est.):     $1,813M
= Standard Owner Earnings:    $123,454M ($5.04/share)
```

*Buffett-Adjusted Owner Earnings (replacing GAAP SBC with actual dilution cost):*
```
Standard Owner Earnings:      $123,454M
+ Add back GAAP SBC:           $6,386M  (already in NI; replacing with true cost)
- Maintenance Buybacks:        $17,550M  (cash to neutralise ~135M shares of annual dilution)
= Buffett-Adjusted OE:       $112,290M ($4.58/share)
```

*Why the adjustment?* GAAP SBC ($6.4B) is recorded at grant-date fair value, but shares vest at much higher prices. Over FY2019-FY2026, $103B in buybacks failed to reduce shares (they actually increased by 64M). The true cost of dilution has been ~4.7x the GAAP expense. Maintenance buybacks are non-discretionary, just like maintenance CapEx.

**1. Gross vs. Net Dilution Trend:** Over FY2020-FY2026, NVIDIA issued approximately 3,864M shares through SBC and repurchased approximately 3,800M shares. **2. The SBC Treadmill Test:** ~64% of cumulative buyback spending ($65.8B of $102.9B) went to anti-dilution. This places NVIDIA firmly in the 🟠 **50-75% range** — the majority of the buyback programme has been an SBC offset mechanism, not a capital return. | Exit P/B Scenario | Rationale | 5-Year CAGR | 7-Year CAGR | 10-Year CAGR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **30.9x (current)** | Multiple sustains | 25.0% | 25.0% | 25.0% |

**The Key Insight — How Much P/B Compression Can You Survive?**

> *"At the current entry P/B of 30.9x with BVPS compounding at ~25% annually, if the P/B multiple remains at 30.9x, the return equals the BV compounding rate of 25%. Every 50% compression in P/B over 10 years creates roughly 6.7% annual drag on returns. The stock can tolerate P/B compression from 30.9x down to approximately 10x before the 10-year return drops below the 10% hurdle rate."*

**The Rule of Thumb for Quick Mental Math:**

```
Approximate Annual Return ≈ (BV Compounding Rate) + Annual P/B Change
where Annual P/B Change = (Exit P/B / Entry P/B)^(1/n) - 1
```

- If P/B stays constant → Return ≈ BV compounding rate (25%)
- If P/B halves over 10 years → ~6.7% annual drag → Return ≈ 25% - 6.7% ≈ 18.3%

## 21. REGULATORY RISKS & POLICY HEADWINDS

### The Core Issue

NVIDIA faces regulatory risk primarily from **US export controls** (Bureau of Industry and Security) and potential **antitrust scrutiny** (FTC/DOJ, EU, France). The current regulatory stance is **mixed** — supportive of domestic AI development but restrictive on China access.

### Historical Regulatory Impact

| Event | Year | Nature | Impact on Company |
|---|---|---|---|
| First China AI chip restrictions | Oct 2022 | Export ban on A100/H100 to China | Developed cut-down A800/H800; ~$5B revenue at risk; largely offset by RoW demand surge |
| Expanded China restrictions | Oct 2023 | Broader chip performance thresholds | Further limited China sales; ~$5B annual impact |
| H20 chip ban & inventory charge | Jan 2025 | H20 (China-specific chip) banned | $4.5B inventory write-down in Q1 FY2026; China DC revenue effectively zeroed |
| French antitrust investigation | Sep 2024 | Competition probe into GPU market | Ongoing; no financial impact to date |

The track record shows NVIDIA has **absorbed regulatory disruptions and thrived** — each restriction initially threatened revenues but was overwhelmed by surging global demand.

### Current Regulatory Headwind Analysis

**1. Nature and Magnitude:** China export controls are a **moderate-to-severe** permanent impairment to one market (~$10-15B annual revenue forgone), but not existential.

**2. Revenue Impact:** Revenue grew 65% in FY2026 despite zero China DC revenue in guidance. Global demand more than compensated.

**3. Competitive Impact:** Export controls affect AMD equally. They may actually STRENGTHEN NVIDIA's moat by forcing Chinese competitors to develop inferior domestic alternatives rather than buying NVIDIA's best chips.

**4. Structural vs. Cyclical:** Structural — China will likely remain restricted for the foreseeable future.

### Demand Curve — Price Sensitivity to Regulatory Costs

| Regulatory Cost Magnitude | Likely Volume Impact | Company's Response | Net Revenue Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mild (5-8% cost — e.g., tariff) | -1% to -3% volume | Pass through to hyperscalers | Neutral to mildly positive |
| Moderate (10-15% — e.g., broader restrictions) | -3% to -5% volume | Develop compliant alternatives; shift to RoW | Flat to mildly negative short-term |
| Severe (>20% — e.g., complete China ban + allies restrict) | -5% to -10% volume | Absorb and rely on domestic/allied demand | **Negative for 1-2 years, then recovery** |

### Risk Rating Table

| Risk Factor | Probability | Impact | Net Risk | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Further China export escalation | Medium | Medium | Manageable | Already largely priced in; China revenue ~zeroed |
| Antitrust action (EU/US) | Medium | Medium | Manageable | Dominance from superior products, not anticompetitive behaviour |
| Tariffs on semiconductor imports | Low-Medium | Low | Manageable | NVIDIA is fabless; TSMC bears manufacturing |
| Forced technology sharing / licensing | Low | High | Low probability | Unlikely under current regulatory framework |

### Buffett's Verdict on Regulatory Risk

**(a) Manageable and Overpriced by the Market.** NVIDIA has survived escalating China restrictions while growing revenue at 65%+. The global AI buildout TAM continues expanding faster than China contracts. The French antitrust probe is immaterial to financials. Regulatory risk is real but not existential, and the market has already substantially priced it in through the China revenue zeroing. If anything, export controls strengthen the moat by preventing Chinese customers from accessing the best chips — forcing them onto inferior domestic alternatives.
