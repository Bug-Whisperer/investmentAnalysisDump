## 1. THE BUSINESS — Understanding the Economic Engine

Eternal Ltd (formerly Zomato Ltd, renamed in March 2025) is the parent company of four operating businesses, each addressing high-frequency consumer needs in India:

1. **Zomato** — Food delivery and restaurant discovery (B2C marketplace; the original business). FY26 Net Order Value (NOV) ~₹36,000+ Cr; ~25.4 million Monthly Transacting Users (MTU) in Q4 FY26.
2. **Blinkit** — Quick commerce (10-minute grocery and essentials delivery). 2,243 dark stores at end of Q4 FY26; NOV ₹14,386 Cr in Q4 FY26 alone (+95% YoY). Acquired in 2022 for ₹4,447 Cr; now the largest contributor to consolidated revenue and the primary growth engine.
3. **District** — A "going-out" super-app launched in current form in FY26 (restaurant bookings, movie/event tickets, playing arenas, local retail discovery). NOV ₹2,736 Cr in Q4 FY26 (+46.5% YoY). Lumpy seasonal business.
4. **Hyperpure** — B2B supply chain for restaurants. Q4 FY26 revenue ₹978 Cr (down ~47% YoY in Q4 alone; full-year FY26 decline more modest ~-19% YoY — largely an accounting effect as inventory now flows through Blinkit).

**Would Buffett understand this business?**

Yes — but with deep reservations. The individual businesses are simple enough to explain to a ten-year-old: "an app for ordering food and groceries." However, Buffett would identify several characteristics that put this firmly **outside his historical circle of competence**:

- **No durable history of earnings.** The combined business has only produced positive Net Income in FY24, FY25 and FY26 — and FY26 NP (₹366 Cr) is *below* FY25 (₹527 Cr). On a true operating basis (EBIT after D&A), the business is **still loss-making at -₹401 Cr EBIT for FY26**. Reported profit exists almost entirely because of ₹1,513 Cr of Other Income generated by deploying ₹17,972 Cr of IPO/QIP cash in treasury instruments. **The operating business is not yet profitable; the treasury portfolio is.**
- **Three of the four businesses are in their land-grab phase.** Blinkit is opening 200+ dark stores per quarter; District is brand-new in current form; only food delivery (Zomato) is stable and modestly profitable. Buffett: *"I don't try to jump over seven-foot bars; I look around for one-foot bars that I can step over."* Forecasting Blinkit's terminal economics is currently a seven-foot bar.
- **Intense, well-capitalised competition.** Swiggy (post-IPO), Zepto (well-funded private), Amazon Now, Flipkart Minutes, Reliance/JioMart, Tata 1mg, BigBasket. Buffett looks for businesses where the moat is widening, not where well-funded competitors are arriving every quarter.
- **The moat is "physical" per management but only partly proven.** CEO Deepinder Goyal explicitly stated: *"Our MOAT is physical"* — citing 17 million sq ft of warehousing, 1 million+ delivery partners, 400,000+ restaurants. This is a real network-effects + supply-chain argument, but the moat is still being built; it has not been tested by a deep credit cycle, a Reliance-led price war that runs for several years, or a regulatory hammer on the gig-economy labour model.

**Business Classification:** Eternal is a **two-sided platform business with strong network effects emerging in food delivery, and a capital-intensive operational machine (dark stores + delivery + supply chain) in quick commerce.** It is **NOT a toll bridge** (no monopoly pricing power), **NOT a consumer franchise** (no Coca-Cola-like brand loyalty that translates into pricing power), and **NOT a switching-cost business** (consumers can install Swiggy/Zepto in 30 seconds). It is closest to a **scaled aggregator with operational moats** — analogous to Domino's Pizza or Costco in its model-DNA, but earlier on the profitability curve.

> Buffett: *"I look for businesses in which I think I can predict what they're going to look like in ten or fifteen years' time. Take Wrigley's chewing gum. I don't think the internet is going to change how people are going to chew gum."* — Eternal flunks this test. The shape of Indian food delivery and quick commerce in 2036 is genuinely unknowable. Will dark stores cannibalise themselves with hyperlocal automation? Will the gig-worker model survive labour law reform? Will Reliance simply outspend everyone? **A Buffett-grade investor cannot answer these questions with high confidence, which means the business sits squarely outside the circle of competence for a concentrated bet.**

### Trend Narrative — The "Profit" That Isn't

Three observations dominate the trend:

**1. The operating business has NEVER been profitable on an EBIT basis.** Despite eight years of revenue growth from ₹466 Cr to ₹54,364 Cr (a 117x increase), EBIT has remained negative in every single year. The "profitability inflection" celebrated in financial press from FY24 onwards is a **GAAP Net Income inflection driven by treasury yield, not an operating inflection.** Strip out Other Income for FY26 and you have:

- Operating Profit (Screener, pre-D&A): ₹1,196 Cr
- Less: Depreciation: ₹1,597 Cr
- = EBIT: **-₹401 Cr**
- Less: Interest: ₹380 Cr
- = Pre-Other-Income PBT: **-₹781 Cr**

Add ₹1,513 Cr Other Income (treasury yield on cash hoard) → PBT ₹732 Cr → NP ₹366 Cr. **The reported "profit" is, in substance, ₹1,513 Cr of interest income on the IPO/QIP cash pile minus an ₹1,147 Cr operating-business loss.** Screener flags this in its own cons list: *"Earnings include an other income of Rs. 1,422 Cr."* (This was the TTM Dec 2025 figure; the FY26 figure is even larger at ₹1,513 Cr.)

> Buffett (2002 Letter): *"Trumpeting EBITDA is a particularly pernicious practice. Doing so implies that depreciation is not truly an expense, given that it is a 'non-cash' charge. That's nonsense. In truth, depreciation is a particularly unattractive expense because the cash outlay it represents has already been made."* Eternal's "adjusted EBITDA" — which the company prominently reports as ₹429 Cr for Q4 FY26 — is **two layers removed from real economic earnings**: it adds back not only D&A but also SBC. For a business spending ₹1,597 Cr/year on D&A (mostly Blinkit dark store leases and equipment) and continuing to issue ESOPs aggressively, this is the "bullshit earnings" Munger warned about.

**2. Margins are improving but not yet at escape velocity.** EBIT margin improved from -174% (FY19) to -0.7% (FY26). The trajectory is genuinely positive — but the business is still slightly EBIT-negative after a decade of operations. Compare this to a true scaling success: Amazon was EBIT-positive (in its retail business, ex-AWS) within five years of its IPO. Eternal is at year five post-IPO and still hasn't crossed the line.

**3. Net Income just declined.** FY25 NP ₹527 Cr → FY26 NP ₹366 Cr (-30.5%). The company is celebrating "346% YoY growth in Q4 FY26 NP" — but the full-year picture shows the headline number going backwards. Reasons: rising D&A from Blinkit's dark-store expansion, rising interest from lease liabilities, and tax rate normalising upwards (24% FY25 → ~50% FY26).

### Buffett's Financial Fortress Test — Adapted

For a normal Buffett analysis, the financial fortress test focuses on (a) low debt, (b) high interest coverage, (c) ample liquidity. Eternal's situation is unusual:

- **Liquidity:** ★★★★★ — ₹17,972 Cr cash post-QIP. The company can survive a 5+ year operating loss scenario without raising another rupee.
- **Debt levels:** ★★★★☆ — Reported D/E of 0.10 looks fine, but the borrowings include lease liabilities (Ind AS 116) on dark stores which are real cash obligations. Net cash position remains strongly positive.
- **Interest coverage:** ★☆☆☆☆ — **Negative**, by definition, because EBIT is negative. The business is paying interest out of treasury income, not out of operating profit.
- **Goodwill / Intangibles:** ⚠️ — Material goodwill from acquisitions, some of which (Paytm Entertainment) appear to have been written down implicitly through the District tech-stack transfer at a fraction of original cost.

> Buffett: *"It is not necessary to do extraordinary things to get extraordinary results."* And: *"A fat wallet is the enemy of superior investment returns."* The first quote applies to Buffett's investee businesses — they should be simple and durable, not feats of financial engineering. The second quote applies to Eternal itself: a ₹17,972 Cr cash hoard is, paradoxically, a problem. It deploys management attention away from the operating business and into capital allocation decisions (where Eternal's track record — Blinkit ₹4,447 Cr, Paytm Entertainment ₹2,048 Cr → ~₹24 Cr — is mixed at best). Worse, the treasury income from this cash is what's enabling the reported "profitability" and obscuring the fact that the **operating businesses, together, still lose money on an EBIT basis**.

**Verdict on Balance Sheet:** Sourced from external capital (IPO + QIP), not generated from the business. A genuine financial fortress is one where the cash was *earned*, not raised. By the strict Buffett definition (Berkshire's float, See's Candies' working capital release), Eternal does not have a financial fortress. It has a **capital cushion** — a meaningful but qualitatively different thing.

### FCF Quality & Conversion from Net Income

**The FCF picture is unflattering on multiple dimensions:**

1. **FCF was negative in 5 of the last 6 years.** Only FY24 produced positive FCF (+₹444 Cr). FY25 reverted to negative (-₹623 Cr) as Blinkit's dark-store expansion accelerated CapEx.

2. **FCF/Net Income conversion is volatile and unreliable.** FY24: NP ₹351 Cr vs FCF +₹444 Cr (126% conversion — looks healthy). FY25: NP ₹527 Cr vs FCF -₹623 Cr (NEGATIVE conversion). The volatility reflects the underlying CapEx cycle for Blinkit's expansion.

3. **"Cash from Operating Activities" itself is partly window-dressed.** Indian Ind AS 116 leases push payment of dark-store rents into financing activities (lease repayments) rather than operating activities. This systematically flatters CFO for asset-light digital companies that lease their physical footprint. The "FCF +₹444 Cr in FY24" should be read with this caveat.

4. **The CFO/Operating Profit ratio shown by Screener (1,747% for FY24) is a meaningless artifact** of dividing CFO ₹646 Cr by tiny Operating Profit ₹43 Cr — not a sign of cash flow quality.

> Buffett (1986 Letter, definition of Owner Earnings): *"These represent (a) reported earnings plus (b) depreciation, depletion, amortization, and certain other non-cash charges less (c) the average annual amount of capitalized expenditures for plant and equipment, etc. that the business requires to fully maintain its long-term competitive position and its unit volume."*
>
> For Eternal, Owner Earnings = NP ₹366 Cr + D&A ₹1,597 Cr − Maintenance CapEx. The hard question is: what is Maintenance CapEx? Eternal's total CapEx (FY25 ~₹931 Cr) is mostly **growth** CapEx for new Blinkit dark stores and warehouses. A reasonable "Maintenance CapEx" estimate is 60-70% of D&A (₹1,000-1,100 Cr) — covering depreciation of existing dark stores, kitchen equipment, IT infrastructure, and lease right-of-use asset replacement. This yields Owner Earnings ≈ ₹845 Cr for FY26. **However**, this OE estimate is generous because it adds back ₹1,597 Cr of D&A while ignoring that EBIT itself is *negative*. A cleaner Buffett reading: **Eternal's true operating Owner Earnings are NEGATIVE** — what's reported as "profit" is treasury yield masquerading as operating performance.

### SBC & Equity Dilution

Eternal does **NOT** conduct buybacks. Instead, it has continuously **diluted** existing shareholders:

> The Buffett/Burry "SBC Treadmill" diagnostic applies here in pure form: Eternal issues equity to employees (real cost, hidden in non-cash SBC line) and does **NOT** buy any back to offset dilution. The diluted share count is therefore rising relentlessly. This is **worse than the NVIDIA case Burry critiqued** — at least NVIDIA spent real cash on buybacks even if they failed to keep up with grants. Eternal doesn't even pretend to offset dilution.

**The IPO and QIP proceeds were largely "value-destructive" in the strict per-share sense** because they were issued at price-to-book multiples well above 1x — meaning the new capital coming in didn't materially increase per-share book value when issued at premium, but it definitely diluted existing per-share earnings claim. The ₹8,500 Cr March 2026 QIP at ~₹240-260 was issued at ~6-7x book value, which is **value-creative** for the company (it raises BVPS) but **dilutive** for existing per-share earnings expectations until the cash earns its keep.

**No dividends.** Cumulative dividends paid since IPO: **₹0**. This is a "growth company" that retains 100% of its (modest) earnings — but as established above, those earnings are predominantly treasury income on the IPO cash, not earned by the operating business.

### CRITICAL: DCF Starting-Point Justification — Why Base OE (Not Dilution-Adjusted) Is Used

> **Per the compliance framework, this is the single most impactful methodological choice in the analysis. Documenting it transparently.**

The template specifies: *"If SBC is material AND company has active buybacks, the DCF must use Dilution-Adjusted Owner Earnings as the starting cash flow."*

For Eternal:
- ✅ **SBC IS material** (273% of FY26 NP — extreme by any standard)
- ❌ **No buyback programme** (zero buybacks in company history)

**Eternal therefore satisfies the SBC materiality criterion but NOT the active-buyback criterion.** The template's "Dilution-Adjusted OE = Base OE − Cash Cost of Anti-Dilution Buybacks" formula is **inapplicable** because there is no actual buyback cash outflow to deduct — the company has chosen to simply absorb the dilution rather than offset it.

**Three possible DCF approaches and the choice made:**

| Approach | Starting OE (₹ Cr) | DCF IV/share (Base) | Honest? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Option 1 — Base OE (no SBC adjustment)** | +845 | ₹78.85 | Understates SBC cost |
| **Option 2 — Dilution-Adjusted OE (notional buyback at market price)** | ~-5,000 | DCF impossible (negative OE → no terminal value calculable) | Overstates impact |
| **Option 3 — Base OE with explicit forward-dilution drag in growth assumptions** | +845 | ₹78.85 (but interpret with caution) | Most defensible |

**Choice made: Option 1 / Option 3 hybrid.** The DCF in Section 15 uses Base OE ₹845 Cr as the starting cash flow (Option 1), BUT interprets the resulting per-share IV with explicit recognition that:

1. **The diluted share count of 965 Cr in the denominator already reflects current dilution**. Future dilution (~4% per year via ESOPs + occasional QIPs) is partially captured in growth assumptions (modest per-share growth despite high company-level growth).

2. **The IV/share figure is the upper bound of fair value** — a more conservative analyst would reduce IV by 15-25% to account for cumulative future dilution over the 10-year horizon. This adjustment would bring Base IV from ₹78.85 to ~₹60-68.

3. **Reader is warned in Section 15** that the analysis uses Base OE for tractability but acknowledges this OVERSTATES intrinsic value.

> **The Buffett-purist alternative** — declaring Eternal "unvaluable" because Dilution-Adjusted OE is negative — is intellectually honest but practically unhelpful. By using Base OE and adjusting the interpretation downward, this analysis preserves comparability with other Buffett-style company analyses while honestly flagging the SBC cost. **Bottom line: the DCF outputs in Section 15 should be considered the OPTIMISTIC bound; true fair value is likely 15-25% lower.**

**Verdict on CapEx Quality:**

Eternal is a **capital-hungry** business, despite the "asset-light digital platform" narrative. The reality is that Blinkit's dark-store network IS the business, and that network is fundamentally physical — not asset-light. CapEx is overwhelmingly Growth CapEx (legitimate from the management's perspective; they are building scale to capture the QC opportunity). The Maintenance CapEx burden is meaningful at ~₹1,000+ Cr/year. The SBC overhang is material — 24 Cr+ shares per year of dilution from ESOPs alone, plus occasional QIPs. **For Buffett-style valuation, the correct lens is to view Eternal's operating business as still EBIT-loss-making, with dilution as a serious additional headwind to per-share economic value.**

### Buffett's Test Applied

Buffett's test for a "great business" is unambiguous: **consistently earns high returns on capital (>15% ROIC/ROCE), can reinvest retained earnings at the same high returns.** Eternal scores 0 of 2 on this:

1. **Not earning above cost of capital.** Cost of equity in India typically 12-14%. Eternal's actual return on equity = 1-2%. **The business is destroying economic value every single year** — it earns less than the cost of the capital deployed in it. Buffett-style "value creation" is the spread between ROE and cost of equity, multiplied by the equity base. For Eternal, this spread is roughly −10 to −12 percentage points × ₹35,000 Cr equity = **~₹3,500-4,000 Cr of annual economic value destruction** (relative to a notional 12% required return).

2. **Cannot reinvest at high rates because there are no high-rate-of-return opportunities being created.** Each year, retained earnings (small) and new equity raises (large) go into Blinkit dark-store expansion. The incremental return on each new dark store is, on a fully-loaded basis (including pre-opening losses, training, advertising, customer acquisition), still **below cost of capital** — which is why consolidated EBIT remains negative even as the network grows.

> Buffett: *"The difference between a good business and a bad business is that good business throws up one easy decision after another. The bad business throws up painful decisions time after time."* — Eternal's decisions have been the painful kind: which Tier-2 city to enter? Which competitor to match in price? When to pull back on marketing? When to deepen dark-store density vs. add new cities? When to pursue an acquisition (Paytm Entertainment, where ₹2,048 Cr investment is being effectively re-priced at ~₹24 Cr just two years later)? A truly great business has a few "what color umbrella to use today" decisions and few existential decisions. Eternal is in the existential-decisions phase.

### What Would Constitute a "Pass"?

For Eternal to become a Buffett-quality compounder, the following would need to happen:
1. Consolidated EBIT turns durably positive (>5% of revenue, not 0% ± 1%) — likely requires Blinkit's contribution margin to lock in above ~10% AND food delivery's profitability to compound.
2. ROCE crosses 15% on a sustained basis — given the asset base, this requires EBIT of ~₹6,000-7,000 Cr (vs current -₹400 Cr). That's a ~15-20x increase from here.
3. The company starts returning cash to shareholders (dividends or buybacks) — signaling that the operating business itself is generating cash beyond what's needed for growth.

None of these has happened yet. Management's own FY29 EBITDA target ($1B = ~₹8,300 Cr) corresponds to Adjusted EBITDA, which after D&A (likely ₹3,000-4,000 Cr by FY29 given current trajectory) and SBC (~₹1,500 Cr) would translate to EBIT of perhaps ₹3,000-4,000 Cr — corresponding to maybe 8-10% ROCE on a then-larger capital base. **Even at management's stretch target, Eternal would only reach the lower end of Buffett-acceptable returns three years from now — and that's assuming flawless execution against well-funded competitors.**

## 10. COMPETITIVE POSITION & MOAT ANALYSIS

### Moat Type Assessment

Eternal has **emerging but not-yet-fortress moats** across its four businesses, of differing types and depths:

**1. Food Delivery (Zomato) — Network Effects + Supply-Side Density:**
- Two-sided platform: more restaurants → more consumer choice → more consumers → more restaurants. Genuine network effect.
- ~400,000+ active restaurants in India; ~25.4 million MTUs; 800+ cities.
- **Real moat exists** but it's a **duopoly moat shared with Swiggy** — not a monopoly. Pricing power is therefore limited (every "platform fee" increase forces Swiggy to match or lose share; commission rates are floored by restaurant pushback). Both companies have struggled to extract more than ~4-5% NPM from food delivery, suggesting the network-effects moat is real but not deep.
- **Vulnerable to:** ONDC (government-backed open network for digital commerce), restaurant-direct-ordering apps (Domino's, Pizza Hut, McDonald's all run their own apps for 30%+ of orders), and the gig-worker labour reform threat.

**2. Quick Commerce (Blinkit) — Physical Supply-Chain Moat (CEO Goyal's claim):**
- 2,243 dark stores, 17 million sq ft of warehousing, supports 1 million delivery partners.
- The physical infrastructure is genuinely hard to replicate quickly — each dark store needs a location, lease, equipment, hiring, training.
- **But the moat is being actively contested:** Zepto has ~1,000+ stores and is well-funded; Swiggy Instamart has scaled rapidly; Amazon, Flipkart, Reliance, and BigBasket (Tata) are all pouring capital in. The "first-mover advantage" is real for ~24 months but not impregnable for 10 years.
- **The capital intensity cuts both ways** — Blinkit's dark-store network creates a defensive cost-to-replicate but it also creates an offensive cost-of-existence (each store has ongoing rent, staff, energy, depreciation). If demand doesn't materialise to fill these stores, they're a fixed-cost trap.
- **Vulnerable to:** A focused, well-capitalised competitor running a multi-year price war (Reliance has the deep pockets for this); regulatory tightening on gig labour; consumer churn to cheaper or faster alternatives.

**3. District — Aggregator Trying to Earn Network Effects:**
- Restaurant booking + movie tickets + events + arenas + local discovery — combining what used to be separate apps.
- **Currently has NO moat.** Competitors include BookMyShow (dominant in movie/event ticketing), Dineout (acquired by Times Internet), and a long tail of category-specific apps.
- District's bet is that having dining + entertainment + Zomato + Blinkit cross-sell creates a "going-out super-app" that is more sticky than any single category leader. Plausible but unproven.

**4. Hyperpure — B2B Supply Chain:**
- A traditional commodity-distribution business with structural challenges (supplier of perishables to restaurants).
- FY26 revenue down 47% YoY (with accounting reshuffles to Blinkit). **No meaningful moat.** The barrier to entry in food-supply distribution is low; pricing power is minimal.

### Critical Moat Questions

**Is the moat enduring or eroding?**
- Food delivery moat: **stable** — duopoly position with Swiggy is fairly settled. Order frequency and AOV are rising.
- Quick commerce moat: **being tested** — well-funded competitors (Reliance, Amazon, Zepto) are scaling aggressively. The "first-mover" advantage is decaying as competitors catch up.
- Going-out super-app moat: **embryonic** — not yet meaningful.

**Market share trends:**
- Food delivery: Eternal (Zomato) has roughly 55% share vs Swiggy 45% — stable for several years.
- Quick commerce: Blinkit has roughly 45-50% share; Zepto ~25-30%; Swiggy Instamart 15-20%; others 5-10%. Blinkit is the share leader but **share is being contested actively**.

**Existential vs manageable competitive threats:**
- **EXISTENTIAL:** A multi-year price war led by Reliance (which can fund any duration of losses) or Amazon (which has structural advantages in fulfillment) could compress Blinkit's economics permanently. ONDC could compress food delivery's commission rates if it scales. Gig-worker regulation could increase the cost base across the entire business by 20-30%.
- **MANAGEABLE:** Zepto's IPO and capital raise — Zepto is a genuine competitor but is also raising public capital at high valuations, meaning they face the same shareholder pressure to slow burn. Swiggy's listing similarly disciplines competition.

**Technological disruption risk:**
- AI chat interfaces replacing apps (CEO Goyal's "MOAT is physical" claim is partly a hedge against this): probably overstated for now. People order food/groceries by habit, not by intelligent recommendation. But this could change in 5-10 years.
- Drone delivery / autonomous delivery vehicles: would commoditise the labour cost and could shift competitive dynamics; current Blinkit has 1 million delivery partners as both a moat and a cost base that could be disrupted.

**Is the business susceptible to change?**
**Yes, profoundly so.** The food delivery and quick commerce business in India 10-20 years from now is **genuinely unpredictable**:
- Will dark stores still exist or will smart fridges + drone delivery replace them?
- Will Reliance simply own the entire quick-commerce category through subsidised pricing?
- Will gig-economy regulation impose a labour cost shock?
- Will an AI super-agent simply book restaurant tables and order groceries on behalf of users, commoditising the consumer-facing brand?

> Buffett: *"We do not get enthused about change, with a few exceptions, as a way to make a lot of money. We're looking for the absence of change to protect ways that are already making a lot of money and even more in the future."* — Eternal's business is the **antithesis** of Buffett's preferred profile. Change is the only constant. The next decade will see massive technological, regulatory, and competitive shifts. A business that needs constant adaptation is the *opposite* of See's Candies (which sells the same chocolate in the same boxes for the same prices for decades).

## 12. MANAGEMENT QUALITY & BUFFETT'S $1 TEST

### Buffett's $1 Test for Eternal

**The $1 Test (Buffett's 1983 framing):** For every $1 of *retained earnings* (cumulative net income minus dividends paid), has the company created at least $1 of market value?

```
Period: FY22 (post-IPO) to FY26 (end of FY26 / start of FY27)
Cumulative Net Income FY22-FY26: ₹(-1222) + ₹(-971) + ₹351 + ₹527 + ₹366 = -₹949 Cr
Cumulative Dividends Paid:        ₹0
Cumulative Retained Earnings:     -₹949 Cr  (NEGATIVE)

Market Cap at IPO (July 2021):    ~₹58,000 Cr (at IPO price ₹76 × ~764 Cr basic shares post-IPO)
Market Cap (13 May 2026):         ₹2,29,500 Cr
Δ Market Cap:                     +₹1,71,500 Cr
```

**Result: Eternal has cumulatively LOST ₹949 Cr of net income since IPO (FY22-FY26), yet market cap has grown by ~₹1,71,500 Cr.** The $1 test is **mathematically undefined** when retained earnings are negative — but the *spirit* of the test is decisive: the market is pricing the business based **entirely on expected FUTURE earnings**, not on a track record of compounding retained earnings into value. The market has effectively said "we're paying ₹1,71,500 Cr for the future earning power we believe is coming." This is **the maximum-risk version of the $1 test outcome** — there is no historical compounding to validate the valuation; the entire valuation is forward-faith.

**Important nuance:** The $1 test was designed for established compounders with positive retained earnings. For a young, post-IPO company with mostly negative cumulative earnings, the test breaks down. But this break-down is itself informative: **Eternal is currently in the phase where Buffett historically passes** — he wants to see at least 5-7 years of demonstrated value creation from retained capital before allocating, and ideally 15-20 years.

### Part A — ROE Quality via DuPont Decomposition

The DuPont identity decomposes ROE into three drivers: **ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier**. This reveals WHERE the return comes from — quality of business (margin × turnover) vs financial engineering (leverage).

¹ FY26 NPM 0.67% and Asset Turnover 1.30 are both distorted by the Blinkit accounting model change (revenue inflated). On LFL revenue ₹32,400 Cr: NPM ~1.13%, AT ~0.78. ### Component Trend Analysis — What Each Driver Tells Us

**Net Profit Margin (NPM) — The Margin Story:**
- FY22 -29% → FY26 +0.7% (a 30-pp improvement over 5 years)
- BUT regressed FY25 → FY26 (2.6% → 0.7%)
- **Driver:** Higher tax rate (24% → ~50%), rising D&A from Blinkit, mostly offset by Other Income growth
- **Quality verdict:** Margins are micro — even the FY25 peak of 2.6% is well below the 8-15% threshold for a quality consumer franchise (ITC: ~25%, HUL: ~17%)

**Asset Turnover (AT) — The Capital-Efficiency Story:**
- FY22 0.32 → FY26 1.30 (4x improvement on reported basis; ~2.5x on LFL basis)
- **Driver:** Revenue scaling faster than asset base growth in early years; Blinkit accounting inflation in FY26
- **Quality verdict:** Genuinely improving — the platform is more revenue-productive per ₹ of assets. This is the strongest positive driver.

**Equity Multiplier (EM) — The Leverage Story:**
- FY22 1.08 → FY26 1.19 (slowly rising)
- **Driver:** Tiny increase in lease liabilities as Blinkit's dark store footprint expands; otherwise the company is essentially unleveraged
- **Quality verdict:** Eternal does NOT use leverage to manufacture ROE. The low ROE is therefore a *pure operating problem*, not a leverage shortfall

### Current Regulatory Headwind Analysis — 6-Point Framework

**1. What is the specific regulatory threat?**
Code on Social Security 2020 implementation that would reclassify gig delivery workers as "employees" or "platform workers" with statutory benefits (PF/EPF, gratuity, ESI, leave encashment).

**2. Probability of materialisation (3-5 year horizon)?**
40-50%. Central government has signalled intent. Multiple state laws already exist. Election cycles may slow or accelerate timing.

**3. Magnitude of financial impact if materialised?**
30-50% increase in delivery cost per order. Eternal has ~1 million delivery partners; even a partial application (employer contribution of 12% to PF + 4% to ESI = 16% on payout) would add ₹3,000-5,000 Cr to annual cost. **This would push Adjusted EBITDA back into negative territory across both food delivery and Blinkit.**

**4. Eternal's mitigation options?**
(a) Pass cost to consumers via platform fees (~15-20% additional headline cost — reduces demand);

(c) Reduce delivery partner numbers via automation (limited near-term — drones/automation are 5-10 years away);

**5. Timing of impact?**
Pre-election (2029): low probability. Post-election (FY30 onwards): medium-high probability. 5-7 years for partial implementation is the central case.

**6. Investor signal/early indicator?**
Watch (a) Karnataka and Rajasthan compliance costs in FY27 (early test of cost impact); (b) Supreme Court litigation outcomes if Eternal/Swiggy challenge state laws; (c) Code on Social Security central rules notification (next 12-24 months).

**Combined lens verdict:** Eternal's regulatory risks are **MANAGEABLE individually**, but the **CUMULATIVE 5-year trajectory of multiple simultaneous regulatory pressures is severe**. None breaks the business; all reduce its margins.
