## 1. THE BUSINESS — Understanding the Economic Engine

Aelea Commodities Ltd, incorporated in 2018 and listed on the BSE SME platform in July 2024, is primarily engaged in **cashew processing and agri-commodity trading**. The business model is straightforward:

1. **Cashew Processing (Core — Value Addition):** The company imports Raw Cashew Nuts (RCN) from African countries (Benin, Tanzania, Burkina Faso, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire), processes them at its facility in Surat, Gujarat, into high-quality kernels and by-products (Cashew Nut Shell Liquid — CNSL), and sells them domestically and internationally under the brands **Tryble** and **Supreme**.

2. **Agri-Commodity Trading (Legacy — Low Margin):** The company trades sugar, rice, wheat flour, pulses, soybean, and onions on a back-to-back (B2B) basis. This segment historically contributed the bulk of revenue (₹496 Cr in FY20) but carries razor-thin margins (~2-4%) and near-zero pricing power.

3. **Subsidiary:** Supreme Commodities DMCC (Dubai) handles international operations.

**Would Buffett understand it?** Yes — buying raw materials, processing them into a finished product, and selling at a margin. This is among the simplest businesses to understand.

**What TYPE of business is it?** This is fundamentally a **commodity processing business** — NOT a consumer franchise, NOT a toll bridge, NOT a switching-cost business. Cashew processing is a relatively low-barrier, price-taking industry. The company has minimal brand equity (Tryble and Supreme are not household names), and its products compete primarily on price and quality, not on brand loyalty. The trading arm is pure commodity intermediation with no value addition.

Buffett: *"Never invest in a business you cannot understand."* The business is understandable, but Buffett would immediately flag: *"I try to invest in businesses that are so wonderful that an idiot can run them. Because sooner or later, one will."* Cashew processing is not that business — it requires active management of raw material sourcing (importing from politically unstable African countries), processing efficiency, and commodity price risk. Margins are thin and volatile.

**Key Concern — Commodity Business Economics:** The company's entire revenue base is subject to commodity price swings. In FY25, operating margins collapsed to ~4.7% (standalone) from ~10.8% in FY24, and net profit cratered to ₹1 Cr from ₹6 Cr. This volatility is characteristic of commodity businesses and is exactly what Buffett avoids.

**Management Pedigree (Notable):** The MD, Hozefa Jawadwala, is a CA with experience at Bank of America, ICICI Bank, Olam International (as CFO, Ivory Coast), ED&F MAN (Sugar division), and Tata International (Agri Division Head). This is unusually strong management pedigree for a BSE SME micro-cap. The promoter, Ashok Patel, founded the company in 2018.

**Processing Capacity:** The company has expanded from 40 MT/day to 140 MT/day of RCN processing capacity and is planning to add 50 MT/day of CNSL Oil manufacturing. This represents a meaningful shift from pure trading (low margin, capital-light) to processing (higher margin, capital-intensive).

### Trend Narrative

The revenue trajectory tells a story of **business model transformation:**

**Phase 1 — Pure Trading (FY20-FY21):** Revenue was ₹496 Cr and ₹391 Cr, but OPM was just 2.8%-3.8%. This was essentially a commodity trading operation with massive topline and wafer-thin margins. Net profit was ₹7 Cr on ₹496 Cr revenue — a 1.4% margin.

**Phase 2 — Transition & Trough (FY22-FY23):** Revenue collapsed to ₹66 Cr (FY22) and ₹80 Cr (FY23). This appears to be when the company shifted focus from pure trading to setting up its own processing unit. OPM swung wildly from -1.5% to 3.8%.

**Phase 3 — Processing-Led Growth (FY24-Present):** With the Surat processing unit operational, revenue rebuilt to ₹120 Cr (FY24) and ₹171 Cr (FY25). OPM peaked at 10.8% in FY24 — a significant improvement reflecting the value addition from cashew processing. However, FY25 saw margin compression back to 4.7%, with net profit collapsing to just ₹1 Cr. The H1 FY26 (Sep 2025) half-year shows strong recovery with ₹174 Cr revenue and ₹9 Cr net profit.

**Key Concern:** Margins are extremely volatile. OPM swung from -1.5% to 10.8% and back to 4.7% over just four years. This is NOT the predictable, steady-state earnings Buffett seeks. The business is clearly sensitive to raw material costs (imported RCN prices), processing efficiency, and commodity market conditions.

Buffett: *"Our favourite holding period is forever."* But holding forever requires predictable economics. With OPM swinging between -1.5% and 10.8%, the economics here are anything but predictable.

### Analysis

**Free Cash Flow has been NEGATIVE in every single year.** This is a critical red flag for a Buffett-style analysis.

- FY25 CFO was a healthy ₹10 Cr (137% conversion of operating profit), but CFI of -₹28 Cr (heavy capex for capacity expansion) resulted in FCF of -₹17 Cr.
- The company funded this gap through CFF of ₹19 Cr (the IPO proceeds and equity issuances).
- FY21 was particularly poor: CFO of -₹13 Cr and FCF of -₹26 Cr.

**Owner Earnings Estimate (TTM):**
- TTM Net Income: ₹6 Cr
- Add back: D&A: ~₹2 Cr (annualised forward run-rate — see methodology note)
- Less: Estimated Maintenance CapEx: ~₹3 Cr (see justification below)
- **Base Owner Earnings: ~₹5 Cr** (₹2.46 per share)

> *Methodology note (D&A and Maintenance CapEx sensitivity): The "~₹2 Cr D&A" figure is the annualised forward run-rate based on H1 FY26's ₹1 Cr half-year D&A (₹1 Cr × 2 = ₹2 Cr/year going forward), reflecting the expanded 140 MT/day facility's expected depreciation. The mechanical TTM sum (H2 FY25 ₹0 Cr + H1 FY26 ₹1 Cr = ₹1 Cr) is lower because H2 FY25 captured the period before the expanded plant was fully operational. Using mechanical TTM D&A of ₹1 Cr would give OE = 6 + 1 − 3 = ₹4 Cr, reducing OEPS to ~₹1.97, raising P/OE to ~77x, and lowering all DCF IVs by ~20% (Optimistic IV would fall to ~₹134, below CMP — eliminating the only scenario with positive MoS). Similarly, the ₹3 Cr maintenance CapEx is calibrated to FY25 fixed assets of ₹49 Cr at 6.1% of gross fixed assets. At Sep 2025's expanded ₹73 Cr asset base, the same 5-7% methodology would imply ₹3.65-5.11 Cr — making OE potentially as low as ~₹2.5-3 Cr (P/OE ~103-124x). Both sensitivities make the AVOID case stronger, not weaker. We retain the stated OE of ₹5 Cr as the analytical baseline because (a) the forward-rate D&A reflects steady-state economics, and (b) maintenance CapEx for a newly-commissioned plant (still under depreciation ramp) is genuinely lower than the long-run 5-7% benchmark suggests. Readers preferring the more conservative mechanical TTM should mentally adjust all DCF IVs down by ~20%; the AVOID conclusion holds under both methodologies.*

**Maintenance CapEx Justification (Business-Specific):** The 140 MT/day Surat processing facility has fixed assets of ~₹49-73 Cr. For a food processing plant with automated shelling/grading equipment, the typical equipment lifecycle is 10-15 years. Annual maintenance replacement therefore runs at ~₹3-5 Cr (5-7% of gross fixed assets). D&A of ₹1-2 Cr likely understates true maintenance needs because the plant is newly expanded — depreciation is low in early years but equipment wear is real from day one (RCN processing involves steam boiling, mechanical shelling, and grading which are physically demanding on equipment). We estimate ~₹3 Cr maintenance (slightly above D&A), reflecting the young but operationally intensive nature of the assets. The remaining ~₹25 Cr of FY25 CFI was clearly growth capex (capacity expansion from 40 to 140 MT/day visible in CWIP → Fixed Assets conversion).

**SBC:** Negligible for this Indian SME — no material stock-based compensation.

The cash flow picture is concerning. The company is in a heavy investment phase (capacity expansion from 40 to 140 MT/day, CNSL oil production), which explains the negative FCF. However, the absence of any positive FCF year in the company's entire history means there is NO track record of cash generation. The business has been funded by borrowings (pre-IPO) and equity issuance (post-IPO).

Buffett: *"Owner earnings represent (a) reported earnings plus (b) depreciation, depletion, amortisation, and certain other non-cash charges less (c) the average annual amount of capitalised expenditures for plant and equipment, etc. that the business requires to fully maintain its long-term competitive position and its unit volume."* By this standard, Aelea's owner earnings are approximately ₹5 Cr — a paltry sum supporting a ₹310 Cr market cap. The implied P/Owner Earnings is ~62x — extremely expensive.

**No dividends have ever been paid.** While Buffett appreciates companies that retain earnings to compound at high rates, this company's ROE (1.9% in FY25) does not justify 100% retention.

**ROE Quality Assessment — Margin-Driven or Leverage-Driven?**

The decomposition reveals a clear story: **pre-IPO ROE (FY20-FY21) was overwhelmingly leverage- and turnover-driven** — the company ran ₹400-500 Cr of trading revenue through a tiny ₹9-15 Cr equity base with equity multipliers of 4-7x. This confirms that ROE here is **margin-fragile, not leverage-fragile** — and margin volatility is structural to the commodity business model, not a one-off. Buffett's preferred profile is high ROE from high margins with low leverage; Aelea has neither high margins (volatile, sub-5%) nor any moat-driven pricing power to sustain them.

Buffett's test: *"A truly great business must have an enduring 'moat' that protects excellent returns on invested capital."* Aelea's returns are neither excellent nor enduring — they fluctuate dramatically with commodity cycles, which is exactly what you'd expect from a business with no pricing power.

## 10. COMPETITIVE POSITION & MOAT ANALYSIS

### Moat Assessment: NO DURABLE MOAT

**Moat Type: NONE (Commodity Business)**

Aelea Commodities operates in an industry with:

- **No brand moat:** Tryble and Supreme are not recognised consumer brands with pricing power. Cashew kernels are largely undifferentiated commodities.

Buffett: *"We do not get enthused about change... we're looking for the absence of change to protect ways that are already making a lot of money."* The industry is indeed stable, but Aelea is NOT making a lot of money from it. The moat is narrow to non-existent.

```
Approximate Annual Return ≈ (ROE × Retention) + Annual P/B Change
where Annual P/B Change = (Exit P/B / Entry P/B)^(1/n) - 1

The current regulatory stance is broadly **neutral to supportive** — India actively promotes agri-processing and exports through schemes like EPCG.
