## 1. THE BUSINESS — Understanding the Economic Engine

Page Industries is the **exclusive licensee** of Jockey International Inc. (USA) for manufacturing, distributing, and marketing the Jockey brand across India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Nepal, UAE, Oman, Qatar, Maldives, Bhutan — and as of FY25, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, and Kuwait. It also holds the exclusive license for Speedo International (swimwear) in India.

**Would Buffett understand this?** Absolutely. This is perhaps the simplest business model in Indian equities: buy cotton yarn → manufacture innerwear and athleisure → sell under one of the world's most trusted innerwear brands → collect a royalty-enhanced margin. A 10-year-old could understand it.

**What type of business is this?** This is a **consumer franchise** — one of Buffett's favourite business types. Jockey's brand in Indian innerwear commands a 10-15% ASP premium over competitors. The product is a daily essential (recession-resistant demand), the brand has exceptional recall, and the switching cost is psychological — once a consumer trusts their innerwear brand, they don't experiment.

Buffett: *"A brand is a promise."* Jockey's promise is comfort, quality, and consistency. That promise has been kept for 147 countries over decades. Page Industries is simply the Indian delivery mechanism for that promise.

**The Economic Engine:**
- **Revenue model:** Manufacture + distribute branded innerwear/athleisure through 1,13,600+ MBOs, 1,556 EBOs, and 1,778 LFS outlets, plus a growing e-commerce channel.
- **Outsourcing:** 36% of production is outsourced, providing cost flexibility.
- **Installed capacity:** 260 million pieces/year across 15 manufacturing units (2.4 million sq ft).
- **Moat type:** Brand franchise + exclusive license (legal moat) + distribution depth + switching costs.

## 8. CAPEX QUALITY ANALYSIS — Growth vs. Maintenance CapEx

> **Owner Earnings Formula (Buffett's preferred metric):**
> **Base Owner Earnings = Net Income + D&A − Maintenance CapEx**
>
> GAAP Net Income already includes all real expenses (including any SBC, which is immaterial here). Owner Earnings does NOT subtract SBC a second time — it starts from NI (which has already absorbed it) and adjusts only for the gap between accounting depreciation and the true cash needed to maintain the asset base.

**Maintenance CapEx Justification (60% of total CapEx):** Page Industries is a brand-and-distribution franchise, not a heavy-industry manufacturer. It outsources 36% of production, and its owned manufacturing is light garment assembly (sewing, cutting, finishing) rather than capital-intensive process plant. Of its total capex, a meaningful portion in FY20-FY24 was **growth capex** — new manufacturing units, capacity expansion to 260 million pieces/year, and warehouse/distribution buildout. Routine maintenance (machine replacement, refurbishment, IT) is the smaller component. ## 10. COMPETITIVE POSITION & MOAT ANALYSIS

**Moat Type: BRAND FRANCHISE + LEGAL EXCLUSIVE LICENSE + DISTRIBUTION DEPTH**

Page Industries possesses a rare multi-layered moat:

1. **Exclusive License Moat (Legal):** The Jockey license for India is exclusive and has been repeatedly renewed/expanded (most recently adding Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Kuwait in FY25). No competitor can sell Jockey in India — this is a legal monopoly on one of the world's most trusted innerwear brands.

2. **Brand Moat:** Jockey commands 10-15% ASP premium over competitors. In innerwear, brand trust matters enormously — consumers are reluctant to experiment with their most personal garments. This creates psychological switching costs.

3. **Distribution Moat:** 1,13,600+ MBOs, 1,556 EBOs, and 1,778 LFS stores — this distribution network took 25+ years to build. Competitors would need billions of rupees and a decade to replicate it.

4. **Cost/Scale Advantage:** 260 million pieces/year production capacity with 36% outsourcing provides cost flexibility. Scale advantages in cotton procurement and manufacturing.

**Key Competitors and Threats:**
- **Lux Industries / Dollar Industries:** Compete in the value segment (₹50-150 price point). Not a direct threat to Jockey's premium positioning (₹200-800+ price point). Manageable threat.
- **Van Heusen Innerwear (Aditya Birla):** Competes in the premium segment. Limited market share gain against Jockey's first-mover advantage. Manageable threat.
- **D2C brands (Taatva, XYXX, etc.):** Digital-first brands targeting millennials/Gen-Z. Growing fast but from a tiny base. Worth monitoring but not existential. The shift of JKYO Groove to 150+ EBOs shows Page is responding.

**Technology / Channel Disruption — Deeper Look:** The disruption risk for Page is a *channel* risk, not a *product-obsolescence* risk. Innerwear itself cannot be technologically disrupted (the product form is stable for decades), so unlike, say, a camera-film or taxi-dispatch business, Page faces no risk of its core product becoming irrelevant. What *is* changing is how innerwear is discovered and bought: (a) e-commerce and quick-commerce (Myntra, Amazon, Flipkart, Blinkit) are taking a rising share of apparel discovery, which lowers the value of Page's physical-shelf moat at the margin and gives digitally-native D2C brands (XYXX, Damensch, Taatva) a lower-cost entry path; (b) marketplace economics can compress margins if Page becomes dependent on third-party platforms that own the customer relationship and charge take-rates. Mitigants: Page's brand pull means customers search for "Jockey" directly (it is not dependent on algorithmic discovery the way an unknown brand is), it has built its own e-commerce and EBO presence, and innerwear's low average ticket size and high replenishment frequency favour trusted incumbents over experimental purchases. Net: a manageable, slow-moving margin/channel risk — not a disruption threat to the franchise itself.
- **Unorganised sector:** Still 60-70% of the Indian innerwear market is unorganised. This is not a threat — it is the TAM. Every percentage point of shift from unorganised to organised benefits Jockey.

**Market Share Trajectory (multi-year):** Page's share of the organised innerwear market and its overall positioning has been broadly stable-to-rising over the past decade. Revenue grew from ₹1,188 Cr (FY14) to ₹4,935 Cr (FY25), a 4.2x increase, against an innerwear market growing roughly 8-10% annually — implying Page has at least held, and modestly gained, share in the premium segment over the period. In premium men's innerwear specifically, Jockey is estimated to hold a leading ~20%+ share, a position that has been durable for 15+ years. The key trajectory nuance: Page's *premium* share is entrenched and stable, while its *total-market* share (~10-11% including the value/unorganised segment) has room to grow as the market formalises. The recent revenue deceleration (FY23-25) reflects a category-wide demand slowdown and post-COVID destocking rather than Page losing share to competitors — peers (Lux, Dollar) decelerated in the same window.

**Susceptibility to Change:** This is the critical Buffett filter. Innerwear is about as change-resistant as businesses get. People will still wear innerwear in 10, 20, 50 years. The basic product form hasn't fundamentally changed in decades. Fashion trends affect outerwear far more than innerwear. Buffett would love this — *"We do not get enthused about change... we're looking for the absence of change."*

**Verdict: WIDE AND DURABLE MOAT.** The combination of exclusive license, brand trust, distribution depth, and an absence-of-change product category creates a moat that is virtually unassailable in the medium term. The only existential risk is non-renewal of the Jockey license — which would be irrational for Jockey International given Page's track record.

## 21. REGULATORY RISKS & POLICY HEADWINDS

Page Industries operates in a relatively benign regulatory environment. The key regulatory bodies are SEBI (securities regulation), Ministry of Textiles (industrial policy), CBIC (customs/GST), and state-level labour authorities. The current regulatory stance is **supportive to neutral** — the Indian government actively promotes textile manufacturing ("Make in India") and organised retail.

### Historical Regulatory Impact

| Event | Year | Nature of Regulation | Impact on Company |
|---|---|---|---|
| GST Implementation | 2017 | Indirect tax reform — unified 5-12% GST replacing complex state taxes | Short-term disruption (1-2 quarters); long-term positive as unorganised competitors lost tax arbitrage. Margins recovered within 6 months. |
| Demonetisation | 2016 | Currency withdrawal — 86% of cash in circulation invalidated | 1-quarter demand shock (cash-dependent retail channel). Recovery within 2 quarters. No lasting damage. |
| COVID Lockdowns | 2020 | Factory shutdowns + retail closures | Revenue fell 3.8% in FY21; fully recovered in FY22 (+37%). Demonstrated resilience of essential product demand. |
| New Labour Codes | 2025 | Enhanced employee benefits, gratuity, social security provisions | One-time ₹35 Cr provision in Q3 FY26. Ongoing cost impact ~2-3% of employee expenses. Fully manageable. |
| US Reciprocal Tariffs | 2026 | 26% tariff on Indian goods entering the US | Indirect impact only — Page has minimal direct exports. Risk is macro demand compression via global slowdown. |

### Current Regulatory Headwind Analysis

The most relevant current headwind is the **New Labour Codes** (effective Nov 2025). This is a **mild** regulatory event.

1. **Nature and Magnitude:** Enhanced employee benefits (gratuity, social security). Severity: Mild.
2. **Demand/Volume Impact:** Zero — labour codes do not affect consumer demand for innerwear.
3. **Revenue Impact:** Nil. This is a cost-side impact only.
4. **Margin Impact:** ~50-70 bps on operating margins on an ongoing basis. One-time provision already absorbed in Q3 FY26.
5. **Competitive Impact:** Affects all organised manufacturers equally. Unorganised competitors face disproportionate burden (less ability to absorb higher labour costs). Net positive for Page's competitive position.
6. **Structural vs. Cyclical:** One-time adjustment, not a structural shift. The business model is fully intact.

### Demand Curve — Price Sensitivity to Regulatory Costs

| Regulatory Cost Magnitude | Likely Volume Impact | Company's Response | Net Revenue Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mild (5-8% cost increase)** | -1% to -2% volume | Price hike to fully absorb; Jockey's brand pricing power supports 5-8% ASP increases | Neutral to mildly positive |

### Buffett's Three-Lens Framework

**1. History as a Guide:** Page has survived GST, demonetisation, and COVID with full recovery each time. No regulatory event has caused lasting damage. The franchise value is resilient.

**2. Elasticity / Pricing Power:** Jockey commands 10-15% ASP premium. Innerwear is inelastic — consumers don't stop buying underwear because of a 5-10% price hike. The unorganised-to-organised shift provides natural volume growth that offsets any price-driven demand destruction.

**3. Terminal Risk Assessment:** There is zero realistic scenario in which regulation permanently destroys the innerwear business. No product ban, no nationalisation, no technology mandate. Innerwear will be manufactured and sold in India for the next 100 years. Terminal risk probability: **negligible**.

Buffett: *"It's only when the tide goes out that you learn who's been swimming naked."* Page Industries has been tested by every regulatory tide of the last decade — and emerged fully clothed each time.
