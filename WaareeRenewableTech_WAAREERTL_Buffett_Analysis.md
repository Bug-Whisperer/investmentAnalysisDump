## 1. THE BUSINESS — Understanding the Economic Engine

Waaree Renewable Technologies Ltd (WRTL) is the **solar Engineering, Procurement & Construction (EPC)** arm of the Waaree Group — India's largest solar module manufacturer. Incorporated in 1999 (formerly Sangam Renewables), the company designs, builds, and commissions ground-mounted and rooftop solar power plants on a turnkey basis. It also develops, finances, owns, and operates a small portfolio of solar projects as an Independent Power Producer (IPP), and provides Operations & Maintenance (O&M) services.

**Business Model — Simple enough for Buffett:**

The core model is straightforward: WRTL wins EPC contracts, procures solar modules (often from parent Waaree Energies at arm's length), constructs the plant, commissions it, and gets paid. It's essentially a **project-based construction business** with a renewable energy tailwind. The economic engine runs on:

1. **Order book execution** — Revenue is driven by the unexecuted EPC order backlog (2.83 GWp as of March 2026, providing 12–15 months of visibility).
2. **Module procurement advantage** — Access to Waaree Energies' 26 GW module manufacturing capacity provides a cost and supply advantage.
3. **Expanding scope** — The company is moving into Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) EPC and, with the planned ₹1,225 Cr acquisition of Associated Power Structures (ASPL), into power transmission and distribution.

**Buffett Classification:** This is NOT a toll bridge, consumer franchise, or switching-cost business. It is a **project-based contractor** operating in a commodity-like industry, but with a **structural tailwind** (India's 500 GW renewable energy target by 2030) and a **parent-company moat** (preferential access to India's largest module capacity). The moat is narrow and comes primarily from the Waaree Group ecosystem rather than standalone competitive advantages.

> *"Never invest in a business you cannot understand."* — Warren Buffett

Buffett would understand this business immediately: it builds solar power plants. The question is whether it's a durable franchise or a cyclical project business riding a wave.

## 10. COMPETITIVE POSITION & MOAT ANALYSIS

**Moat Type:** Narrow — primarily an **ecosystem/relationship moat** derived from the Waaree Group.

**Moat Sources:**
1. **Parent Group Integration:** Access to India's largest solar module manufacturing capacity (26 GW Waaree Energies) provides supply certainty and potential cost advantages in module-inclusive EPC contracts.
2. **Track Record & Execution Capability:** 2.83 GWp order book and demonstrated ability to execute large utility-scale projects builds reputation.
3. **O&M Portfolio:** Growing O&M portfolio creates recurring revenue and customer stickiness.

**Moat Weaknesses:**
1. **EPC is inherently competitive.** Barriers to entry are moderate — capital requirements are low, and projects are won through competitive bidding. Margins are under constant pressure.
2. **No pricing power.** WRTL is a price-taker in competitive bids. It cannot raise prices without losing orders.
3. **Parent dependency.** A significant portion of business likely flows through the Waaree Group ecosystem. Standalone competitive advantage without the parent is unclear.
4. **Project-based revenue.** Unlike recurring revenue businesses, WRTL must continuously win new orders. A slowdown in tendering or government policy shifts could hit order inflows.
