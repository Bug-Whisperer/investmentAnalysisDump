## 1. THE BUSINESS — Understanding the Economic Engine

Tanfac Industries Ltd, incorporated in 1972 and commencing commercial production in March 1985, is **India's largest supplier of fluorine-based chemicals**. It is a **joint venture** between **Anupam Rasayan India Limited (ARIL)** and the **Tamil Nadu Industrial Development Corporation (TIDCO)**. The company manufactures:

- **Anhydrous Hydrofluoric Acid (AHF)** — the core building block
- **Dilute Hydrofluoric Acid (DHF)** — including solar-grade DHF
- **Aluminium Fluoride, Potassium Fluoride, Potassium Bifluoride**
- **Sulphuric Acid, Oleum**
- **Boron Trifluoride Complexes, Specialty Fluorinated Chemicals**

The business model is a **chemical toll bridge with switching costs**: Tanfac processes fluorspar (the key raw material) into hydrofluoric acid and downstream derivatives that serve multiple end-markets — solar cells, steel surfacing, refrigerants, glass etching, agrochemicals, fluoropolymers, lithium batteries, and pharmaceuticals. Fluorine chemistry is hazardous and requires specialised handling, creating **high barriers to entry** through regulatory permits, safety expertise, and customer qualification.

> *Buffett: "I want a business so good that an idiot can run it, because sooner or later, one will."*

Tanfac is not quite that simple — fluorine chemistry requires genuine technical competence — but the business model IS simple to understand: buy fluorspar, process it into HF acid and derivatives, sell to industrial customers on long-term contracts. The economics are driven by **volume × realisation × capacity utilisation**. Buffett would understand this as an **essential industrial input supplier with a cost-advantage moat** — there are very few producers in India, and the product is critical to multiple downstream industries.

**Key Developments:** ARIL acquired management control in FY23. Under new management, the company has embarked on aggressive capacity expansion:
- Expanded HF capacity ~6x from 4,850 TPA to 29,700 TPA in FY25 (new DHF unit commissioned Oct 2024)
- Commissioned first phase of 10,000 TPA Solar Grade DHF in June 2025
- Approved ₹495 Cr new downstream fluorinated chemicals facility (20,000 TPA) — commissioning by Nov 2026
- Secured ₹2,362 Cr 7-year supply contract with Japanese customer (₹337.5 Cr/year from Jan 2027)
- Board approved ₹500 Cr QIP fundraise; 1:2 stock split completed May 2026

### Trend Narrative

The transformation is dramatic. From **loss-making** in FY14-FY16 with EBIT margins of 2-3%, the company has pivoted to a **highly profitable specialty chemical business** generating 17-22% EBIT margins and 14-16% net margins. Key observations:

1. **Revenue acceleration**: 10-year revenue CAGR of 17%, but the recent trajectory is steeper — FY25 revenue of ₹557 Cr was up 47% YoY.

2. **EBIT margin resilience (through FY25)**: EBIT margin stabilised in the 17-22% band from FY19 through FY25. Both OPM (pre-D&A) and EBIT margin track closely because D&A is modest relative to revenue — indicating the business was NOT capital-intensive in steady state. FY25 OPM of 23.5% vs EBIT margin of 21.7% — the 1.8pp gap is small. *Caveat: This capital-light characterisation and the 17-22% range apply to the pre-expansion steady state. As of the most recent quarters (Jun-Dec 2025), EBIT margins have compressed to 12-15%, below the historical floor — see Section 6 for detail. TTM D&A has risen to ₹17 Cr (vs FY25 ₹10 Cr), widening the OPM-EBIT gap to ~2.9pp. The business is becoming more capital-intensive as the new capacity depreciates.*

3. **Earnings power inflection**: Net profit surged from ₹52 Cr (FY24) to ₹88 Cr (FY25), a 69% jump. The 5-year EPS CAGR of **39%** is exceptional.

4. **Zero dilution**: Shares have remained constant at 199.52 lakh (post-split) throughout. The proposed ₹500 Cr QIP would be the first equity raise.

> *Buffett: "The best business to own is one that over an extended period can employ large amounts of incremental capital at very high rates of return."* Tanfac is entering exactly this phase — EBIT surged 86% in FY25 as new capacity came online, validating that incremental capital earns exceptional returns.

### Financial Fortress Assessment

Tanfac's balance sheet transformation is one of the most remarkable in Indian small-cap history:

- **From technically insolvent to fortress**: Shareholders' equity was effectively **zero** in FY15-FY16. By FY25, equity stands at ₹312 Cr.

- **Debt eliminated, now selectively re-leveraging**: Total debt went from ₹70 Cr (FY14) to **zero** by FY20. The ₹41 Cr in FY25 is fresh borrowing for capacity expansion — at a D/E of just 0.13x and Debt/EBIT of 0.3x. Interest coverage of 24.2x (on EBIT, not EBITDA) means the company can service its debt more than 24 times over from operating profits alone.

- **Net Debt/Owner Earnings of 0.4x**: The company could repay its entire net debt from just 5 months of owner earnings. This is a **financial fortress** by any standard.

> *Buffett: "We want the business to be one that produces cash, not consumes it."* With zero goodwill, near-zero debt, and interest coverage of 24x, Tanfac meets Buffett's fortress standard on the balance sheet. *However, the fortress characterisation requires caveat: FY25 FCF was −₹64 Cr (the most negative in company history), and the ₹500 Cr QIP requirement shows that internal cash generation is insufficient for the expansion plan. The balance sheet is a fortress; the cash flow statement is currently in investment mode.*

### Cash Flow Commentary

Tanfac's FCF turned sharply negative in FY24-FY25 — this reflects **aggressive growth investment**, not business deterioration. ### Key Observations

1. **Revenue Trajectory**: Revenue peaked at ₹178 Cr in Dec-24 (Q3FY25) when new DHF capacity was operating at high utilisation. Recent quarters (Jun-Dec 2025) show revenue stabilising at ₹169-176 Cr — still strong but moderating from the peak.

> *"In the business world, the rearview mirror is always clearer than the windshield."* — Warren Buffett

The Dec-25 quarter (EBIT margin 12.1%, NP down 54% YoY) approaches "Deteriorating" territory. However, the label "Decelerating" rather than "Deteriorating" is used because: (1) revenue has not declined on a TTM basis (TTM ₹690 Cr vs FY25 ₹557 Cr — still growing); (2) the margin compression is believed to be post-commissioning normalisation rather than structural erosion (the initial high-utilisation quarters after Oct-24 commissioning were abnormally profitable); and (3) the Japanese contract (₹337.5 Cr/year from Jan 2027) provides a specific, identifiable recovery catalyst. **If EBIT margins remain below 15% for two more quarters (Mar-26, Jun-26) without the Japanese contract providing uplift, this assessment should be upgraded to "Deteriorating."**

## 21. REGULATORY RISKS & POLICY HEADWINDS

### The Core Issue

Tanfac operates under multiple regulatory frameworks: **Factories Act** and **Manufacture, Storage and Import of Hazardous Chemicals Rules** (HF acid is classified as hazardous); **Pollution Control Board** environmental clearances; **BIS standards** for product quality; and general **SEBI/Company Law** governance.

**Current regulatory stance: Supportive.** India's government has actively promoted domestic chemical manufacturing through PLI schemes, anti-dumping duties on Chinese imports, and "Make in India" initiatives.

### Historical Regulatory Impact

| Event | Year | Nature | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|

| GST implementation | 2017 | Tax reform | Neutral — transition disruption, then normalised |

### Buffett's Three-Lens Framework

1. **History as Guide**: Tanfac has operated for 40+ years under India's hazardous chemicals regulatory regime. No material adverse regulatory events.
2. **Pricing Power**: As an essential input supplier with few alternatives, Tanfac can pass through regulatory compliance costs to customers.
3. **Terminal Risk**: Negligible — there is no realistic scenario where India bans fluorine chemistry. Solar, battery, and semiconductor industries REQUIRE HF acid.
