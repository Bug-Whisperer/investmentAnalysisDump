# Multibagger Hunt: Indian Micro/Small-Cap Equities via SMILE + Lynch GARP + Buffett–Munger Quality (July 2026)

## TL;DR
- **Two names clear all three lenses today: Shilchar Technologies (transformers) and HBL Engineering (railway Kavach) — both are high-ROCE, debt-free, promoter-heavy businesses riding structural, multi-year tailwinds, but both have already partly re-rated, so entry discipline matters.** The single best "still-neglected + quality" combination is Shilchar (Buy on dips); HBL is a Watch pending two quarters of clean execution after its Q4 FY26 margin wobble and CLW tender loss.
- The richest structural tailwinds are power T&D/transformers (a stated **over ₹9,15,000 crore transmission investment opportunity to 2032** under the National Electricity Plan), railway safety indigenisation (Kavach), electrification components (smart meters, shunts, EV busbars), EMS (India's EMS market ~$40–45bn in FY25 heading toward ~$150bn by FY30), and precision aerospace/defence engineering.
- Valuation is a lens, not a gate: on a 10–12% personal hurdle several of these names still imply beatable growth, but a few (Azad Engineering at ~70–100x, Avalon at ~75–100x) already discount terrific outcomes and move to Watch/Avoid. Micro-caps carry high volatility and liquidity risk; this is a research starting point, not investment advice.

## Part A — Sectors (top-down tailwinds)

**1. Power Transmission & Distribution / Transformers.** India's National Electricity Plan (Transmission), launched 14 October 2024, "provides visibility to the investors of the massive investment opportunity of over INR 9,15,000 Crores in Transmission Sector till the year 2032" (Ministry of Power / CEA, PIB), to support 500 GW of renewables by 2030 and 600+ GW by 2032. Add a "historic surge" in US/Europe transformer demand (grid upgrades, data centres, EV charging, ageing-asset replacement) creating a global demand–supply mismatch and longer lead times (Motilal Oswal, FY26). Runway: at least FY25–FY28 on domestic capex alone, longer with exports. Early-to-mid cycle; ordering was temporarily soft in FY26 (16 schemes vs 45 in FY25) on execution bandwidth, not demand. TAM: India transformer market in the multiple-billion-dollar range, with large (>100 MVA) units growing fastest (~9.75% CAGR to 2031, Mordor Intelligence).

**2. Railway Safety Indigenisation (Kavach ATP).** Railways targets Kavach across 44,000 km (Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw, 30 July 2025) — but as of March 2026 only ~3,103 route-km were commissioned with work underway on ~24,427 route-km (roughly 4.4% of the ~70,000 route-km network), so this is very early cycle with a long runway. Only three certified OEMs (HBL, Kernex, Medha). Record FY26 railway budget allocation. Phased, multi-year tenders through the early 2030s.

**3. Electrification & Metering Components.** Under RDSS, 20.33 crore smart meters are sanctioned (covering 19.79 crore consumers), but **only ~3.46 crore were installed as of 30 June 2025** and the deadline was extended from March 2026 to March 2028 (MoS Power, Rajya Sabha) — i.e. the bulk of the rollout is still ahead. Shunt resistors, bimetals, and EV busbars ride EVs + smart meters + battery management. Long multi-year runway.

**4. Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS).** Per KPMG in India's report *"India's Electronic Manufacturing Services (EMS) Opportunity"* (8 June 2026), "India's EMS market has scaled rapidly to around USD 40-45 billion in FY25 and is expected to surpass around USD 150 billion by FY30"; India is only ~5–6% of the ~$640–650bn global market, aided by ~$19.5bn of cumulative incentives. Freshly boosted by the Union Cabinet's 15 July 2026 approval of **Semicon 2.0 (₹1,27,500 crore) plus the Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme (₹62,500 crore) — a combined ~₹1.9 lakh crore (~US$22bn)** package. Runway: 5–10 years. Caveat: KPMG's "scale without depth" paradox — 80–95% import dependence on critical components, thin margins in commodity assembly.

**5. Precision Aerospace / Defence / Energy Engineering.** India targets ₹3 lakh crore of indigenous defence production by FY2028-29 (Defence Minister Rajnath Singh, 18 April 2025; FY25 output ₹1.54 lakh crore); the FY26 MoD budget is ₹6,81,210 crore, with ₹1,11,544.83 crore (75% of the modernisation budget) reserved for domestic procurement (PIB, 1 Feb 2025). Global gas-turbine and aerospace supply chains are re-shoring to qualified Indian precision machiners. Runway: 5–10 years; high entry barriers (long qualification cycles).

**6. Import-substitution niche industrials (coding/marking, specialty components).** Formalisation + QCO/BIS standards favour organised domestic players. Steady, less cyclical.

## Part C — Deep Dives

### 1. Shilchar Technologies Ltd (BSE: 531201 / NSE: SHILCTECH) — **BUY (on dips)**

**(1) Business model.** Shilchar, based in Vadodara, makes power & distribution transformers (the growth engine) plus legacy electronics/telecom transformers. It serves renewables (inverter-duty/solar), utilities, and industrial EPC clients, and exports ~48% of revenue (FY26), with the US ~18–19% and Middle East ~30%. It commands a niche premium position in customised, high-efficiency transformers, competing with Voltamp, Transformers & Rectifiers, Bharat Bijlee and Danish Power. Moat: decades of qualification, customer stickiness in exports, low-cost high-quality manufacturing, and a debt-free balance sheet that lets it self-fund expansion. Unit economics are excellent — ~29% EBITDA margins with negligible interest cost.

**(2) Scorecard (all figures dated).**
- Market cap ~₹5,020 cr; share price ~₹4,390 (early July 2026); trailing P/E ~32x.
- Ownership: Promoters 62.12% (Mar 2026, down from 64.01% — a mild negative), FIIs 2.55%, DIIs 1.91% — genuinely under-owned by institutions. NSE listing only began Nov 24, 2025, widening the investable window.
- Revenue: FY24 ₹397 cr → FY25 ₹623 cr → FY26 ₹651.94 cr. PAT: FY24 ₹92 cr → FY25 ₹146.85 cr → FY26 ₹158.16 cr. FY21–25 CAGRs: revenue ~38%, PAT ~83% (May 2026 concall). FY26 growth slowed (Q4 hit by deferred, not cancelled, Middle East shipments amid West Asia logistics disruption).
- ROCE FY26 ~51% (down from ~71% FY25 as equity/cash built up); ROE ~38%. Debt-free; net cash ~₹246 cr at March 2026.
- Cash quality: cumulative FY22–FY26 CFO ₹341 cr vs PAT ₹454 cr ≈ 75% conversion; FY24–FY26 ≈ 78%. FY26 CFO ₹192 cr (121% of PAT) after a weak FY25 (27%) — lumpy but cumulatively healthy.
- Working capital: cash conversion cycle ~105 days FY26 (improved from ~120); Screener flags a jump in "working capital days" driven largely by cash parked in investments (Investments rose ₹54→₹202 cr), not core operating deterioration.
- Dilution: minimal (a 2:1 bonus June 2025 is not dilutive); growth self-funded from internal accruals.
- SMILE: **S** — ₹652 cr revenue in a multi-billion-dollar TAM = long runway. **M** — promoter family (Shah) has run the business since 1986, executed multiple capacity expansions. **L** — targeting 220 kV/160 MVA class and ~₹1,400–1,500 cr revenue by FY29–30. **E** — over ₹9,15,000 crore transmission opportunity + global transformer shortage.
- Governance/forensic: Debt-free; unmodified audit opinion (CNK & Associates LLP); ₹12.50 dividend; only a minor ₹3.2 cr GST demand (being challenged). Other income modest. Clean.

**(3) Valuation (lens).**
- Reverse DCF: At ~₹5,020 cr mcap and ~₹158 cr FY26 PAT (~32x), the price implies roughly low-to-mid-teens FCF growth for a decade at a 10–12% discount rate; at 15% the implied required growth rises toward high-teens. Management guides FY27 revenue ₹800 cr (+23%) at 29–31% margins, and ~₹1,400–1,500 cr long-term — so ~15–20% earnings CAGR is realistically achievable from capacity (14,000 MVA post-expansion) × utilisation × pricing. Implied growth is beatable → lean Buy.
- Optical-illusion check: Expensive on Q4-depressed TTM but cheaper on FY27/FY28 as the Gavasad-3 expansion (6,500 MVA, ~₹120 cr internally funded, commissioning April 2027) volume and 220 kV entry normalise the base. Hidden opportunity, not a value trap — Q4 weakness was logistics-driven and export shipments deferred, not lost.
- Forward DCF (light sanity, 5–7 yr, three rates): Bear ~₹3,600–4,000 (margin compression to mid-20s, export stall); Base ~₹5,500–7,000 (₹800 cr FY27 scaling to ₹1,200 cr+, 29% margins); Bull ~₹8,000–10,000 (full ~₹1,500 cr, 30%+ margins, re-rating on institutional discovery). The range widens as the discount rate moves from 10% (top of each band) to 15% (bottom). Terminal assumptions sensitive to CRGO steel/copper prices and export tariffs.
- Napkin math: If PAT roughly doubles to ~₹300 cr by FY29–30 and the multiple holds ~30x (or re-rates modestly as institutions enter), mcap ~₹9,000 cr — a ~1.8x; a re-rating to 35–40x on an institutional-discovery narrative plus earnings could deliver a ~2.5–3x over 4–5 years.
- 10-year-old test: "This company makes the boxes that move electricity around. India and the world both need way more of them and can't make enough; this company has no debt, earns terrific returns, and is building a big new factory paid for with its own cash."

**(4) Verdict — BUY on dips.** Target range ₹5,500–7,000 (base, 12–18 month) with upside to ₹8,000+ on discovery; margin of safety wants entry nearer ₹4,000–4,300 (implied downside ~10–12% to that zone, upside ~25–55% to base). **Discount rate I stand behind: 12%** — a genuinely high-quality, debt-free business with a moat and huge market, but with export/commodity cyclicality and single-family concentration that don't justify the full 10%. (Not using 15%, so no double-counting issue; at 10% the case is clearly Buy.) Re-rating catalyst: FY27 volume ramp + 220 kV entry + institutional/index discovery as it sits right at the ₹5,000–6,000 cr institutional-radar threshold. **Discovery runway:** newly NSE-listed (Nov 2025) with only ~2.5% FII + ~1.9% DII, the path from here is capacity commissioning (FY27) → earnings step-up → sell-side initiation and MF/index entry into the deep public float (~33%). Kill criteria (business/governance, not DCF): sustained EBITDA margin below ~24%, export share collapsing, promoter selling accelerating, or any audit qualification. Key risks: copper/CRGO volatility, export tariff/logistics shocks, order-book opacity (limited backlog disclosure).

### 2. HBL Engineering Ltd (NSE: HBLENGINE) — **WATCH**

**(1) Business model.** Formerly HBL Power Systems, HBL runs four segments: railway electronics (Kavach ATP — the growth engine), industrial batteries (legacy anchor), defence & aviation batteries, and others. It is one of only three certified Kavach vendors and was first to receive RDSS approval for Kavach 4.0, giving a durable regulatory moat in a government-mandated safety rollout. Q4 FY26 electronics revenue jumped ~229% YoY.

**(2) Scorecard.**
- Market cap ~₹20,500 cr; P/E ~24x; trading ~9.3x book.
- Ownership: Promoters 59.1%, zero encumbrance (Mar 2026). Larger and more discovered than a classic micro-cap.
- FY26: revenue ~₹3,303 cr, PAT ~₹814 cr (up ~198%); net margin ~24.6%. ROCE ~58%, ROE ~45%; debt/equity ~0.03.
- Order book ~₹4,000 cr+ (Kavach), providing multi-year visibility; FY27 Kavach sales guided ~₹1,900 cr. Recent large wins: ₹1,714 cr from CLW (May 2026), ₹800 cr from BLW.
- Concern: Q4 FY26 EBITDA margin fell to 12.4% from 16.7% on execution costs; company lost the CLW 6,300-loco tender on pricing (Jan 2026), sending the stock down 14% that day.
- SMILE: **S** — now mid-cap, less runway on size. **M** — proven management. **L** — credible Kavach expansion. **E** — 44,000 km TAM (only ~3,100 km commissioned so far).
- Governance: Clean, zero pledge, arm's-length order disclosures (explicitly non-related-party).

**(3) Valuation.** Reverse DCF at ~24x on ₹814 cr PAT implies high-teens growth is needed; achievable if Kavach scales to ₹1,900 cr FY27, but the CLW loss and margin dip inject execution risk. Optical-illusion: FY26 PAT may be flattered by lumpy order execution; watch for normalisation. Napkin: if Kavach drives PAT to ~₹1,200 cr by FY28 at ~22x, mcap ~₹26,000 cr — modest ~1.3x from here, so much of the story is priced.

**(4) Verdict — WATCH.** The business quality is A-grade (58% ROCE, debt-free, moat), but at ~₹20,500 cr it is past the "neglected micro-cap" stage and the Q4 margin/tender wobble warrants two clean quarters before Buy. Discount rate: **12%**. Moves to Buy on two consecutive quarters of margin recovery (EBITDA back above ~16%) plus a fresh large Kavach win. Kill criteria: repeated tender losses, margin staying sub-13%, battery-segment drag. Risks: government capex dependence, working-capital scale-up, competitive pricing.

### 3. Danish Power Ltd (NSE SME: DANISH) — **WATCH**

**(1) Business model.** Jaipur-based, four-decade transformer maker specialising in inverter-duty transformers (IDT ~70% of revenue) for solar/wind, plus power/distribution transformers and control-relay panels. Estimated ~10–11% share of India's IDT installations (~14 GW of ~120 GW solar). Clients include Tata Power, ABB, Torrent, Greenko. A 14-year IDT head start is its moat. Competes with Shilchar, Wilson, CG, Atlanta.

**(2) Scorecard.**
- Market cap ~₹1,720 cr; P/E ~18–24x (well below industry ~44x).
- Ownership: Promoters 73.6% (high, aligned) — but very tight float and NSE-SME listing = serious liquidity constraint.
- FY26: revenue ₹521 cr (+22%), PAT ~₹69 cr (+26%); ROE ~18%, ROCE ~23%; near debt-free (gearing ~0.01x). H1 finance costs down ~69% YoY.
- Order book crossed ₹500 cr; first European export order (~₹84 cr, 66 kV) secured. H2-heavy seasonality.
- Concern: working-capital days rose from 95 to 150; CRISIL flagged debtor/inventory build.
- SMILE: **S** — small, long runway. **M** — 40-year operator. **L** — moving to 220 kV EHV segment. **E** — solar + transformer shortage.

**(3) Valuation.** Cheapest transformer play on P/E; reverse DCF implies only modest growth needed, and IDT + EHV entry make mid-teens+ growth achievable → attractive on paper. But SME-segment illiquidity means institutions largely cannot build positions until it migrates to the main board.

**(4) Verdict — WATCH (Buy candidate on main-board migration).** The valuation and returns are appealing, but the NSE-SME listing and thin float violate the investability requirement — no fund can take a meaningful position today. Discount rate: **12–15%** (SME liquidity + working-capital stretch justify the higher end; double-counting caveat — at 12% the value case is stronger, so I anchor the call to the *liquidity* gate rather than the DCF). Re-rating catalyst: migration to NSE main board (index/institutional access), 220 kV validation, and working-capital normalisation. Kill criteria: working-capital days continuing to climb, IDT margin compression from new entrants, promoter selling. This is the classic "on the edge of discovery" name.

### 4. Shivalik Bimetal Controls Ltd (NSE: SBCL) — **WATCH**

**(1) Business model.** Manufactures thermostatic bimetal/trimetal strips, shunt resistors, and clad metals for circuit breakers, smart meters, EVs and industrial controls. Global #2 in thermostatic bimetal (~15–16% share vs EMS Metals' 40–45%). Components now ~60–70% of revenue (0% in FY18) as it moves up the value chain to finished precision parts and sub-assemblies (Smart DC Current Sensor modules for BMS/ESS). Serves 300+ clients globally; long qualification cycles are the moat.

**(2) Scorecard.**
- Market cap ~₹3,660 cr; P/E ~35–38x; ~8.7x book.
- Ownership: Promoters only 33.2% (a clear negative on skin-in-the-game, though zero pledge), DIIs ~21%, FIIs ~3.6% — DIIs already meaningfully present, so less "neglected."
- FY26: revenue ~₹462–571 cr, PAT ~₹95.8 cr (+25%); ROCE ~26%, ROE ~21%; debt/equity ~0.08.
- Growth drivers: RDSS smart-meter rollout (20.33 cr sanctioned, only ~3.5 cr installed to mid-2025 — long tail ahead), EV busbar facility (₹20 cr capex → ₹250–300 cr revenue over 3 yrs), Vishay inventory normalisation, US tariff-driven shift from strips to finished components (46% Europe bimetal growth FY26).
- SMILE: **S** — small. **M** — since 1984, deep materials engineering. **L** — busbar/sub-assembly expansion. **E** — electrification.

**(3) Valuation.** At ~36x, reverse DCF implies mid-teens growth needed; achievable given EV + smart-meter tailwinds and capacity headroom (₹1,300–1,600 cr ceiling vs ₹571 cr FY26). But 33% promoter holding and prior demand lumpiness (Vishay) temper conviction.

**(4) Verdict — WATCH.** High-quality niche compounder, but low promoter holding and already-present DIIs reduce both the alignment comfort and the "under-owned" edge. Discount rate: **12%**. Moves to Buy on evidence of sustained shunt/busbar volume acceleration and Vishay normalisation at a better entry multiple. Kill criteria: promoter stake falling further, EV/smart-meter demand disappointing, margin erosion. Risks: customer concentration (Vishay), commodity (silver/copper) input volatility.

### 5. Genus Power Infrastructures Ltd (BSE: 530343 / NSE: GENUSPOWER) — **WATCH (governance-gated)**

**(1) Business model.** India's leading smart-meter maker (18M+ meter capacity), now a pure-play after spinning off its investment arm. Executes AMISP concessions under RDSS; expanding into gas/water meters.

**(2) Scorecard.**
- Market cap ~₹9,400 cr; P/E ~16x (cheap vs peers).
- Ownership: Promoters fell to ~39.4% (down ~11% over 3 yrs — a negative); FIIs rose sharply to ~18.9% (Mar 2026) — no longer neglected.
- FY26: revenue ~₹4,751 cr (+94%), PAT ~₹592–605 cr (+93%); ROCE ~19%, ROE ~18%; debt/equity ~0.73.
- Order book ₹25,173 cr (2.5x mcap) — huge visibility; FY27 guidance ₹6,000–6,500 cr.
- **Governance flag:** Enforcement Directorate search (Dec 2024) at office and Chairman's home under money-laundering law; a related court notice in the March quarter. Company asserts full compliance.

**(3)/(4) Verdict — WATCH, governance-gated.** Cheap, huge order book, and strong tailwind, but the ED investigation is an unresolved governance overhang, promoter holding is falling, and FIIs already own ~19% (the neglect thesis is gone). Discount rate: **15%** (governance/leverage risk genuinely elevates the hurdle — this is risk-based, not a reflexive micro-cap penalty; at 12% it would screen cheap, but the governance gate, not the DCF, caps the call). Kill criteria: adverse ED/court outcome, further promoter selling, working-capital blowout. Only for those comfortable underwriting the legal risk.

### 6. Azad Engineering Ltd (NSE: AZAD) — **AVOID (valuation-gated to Watch)**

**(1) Business model.** Hyderabad precision manufacturer of mission-critical forged/machined components (3D turbine airfoils/blades) for aerospace, defence, energy and oil & gas OEMs (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi, Honeywell, Pratt & Whitney). ₹6,500 cr+ long-term order book; a genuine high-barrier niche.

**(2) Scorecard.**
- Market cap ~₹15,000 cr; P/E ~70–100x.
- FY26: revenue ~₹590 cr (+30%), PAT ~₹133 cr (+49%), EBITDA margin ~37%. But **ROCE only ~10–15%, ROE ~8–9%**; borrowings nearly doubled to ₹451 cr; **operating cash flow negative ~₹123 cr FY26** on a ~₹300 cr working-capital build.
- SMILE fit strong on aspiration/market, weak on returns/cash today.

**(3)/(4) Verdict — AVOID at current price (Watch on de-rating).** The business is A-grade strategically, but it fails the Buffett cash/returns test right now (single-digit ROE, negative CFO) and the reverse DCF at ~70–100x demands flawless hypergrowth for a decade — the market already discounts terrific things. Discount rate: **15%** justified by unproven-at-scale returns and heavy working capital — and here the high implied growth is a genuine over-discount signal, not double-counting, because returns/cash are objectively weak (verified: negative FY26 CFO, sub-10% ROE). Re-rating/entry only if ROCE crosses ~18%, CFO turns solidly positive, and the multiple compresses. Kill criteria: CFO staying negative, ROCE not improving as capacity ramps.

## Part D — Watchlist / Rejected

- **Avalon Technologies (₹11,500 cr):** Excellent 46% FY26 revenue growth and 20.6% ROCE, order book ₹2,196 cr (+25%), but ~75–100x P/E, negative levered FCF (~–₹40 cr TTM), and rising inventory/receivables. **Watch on de-rating** — valuation moves it off Buy.
- **Control Print (₹1,040 cr):** Zero-debt coding/marking near-monopoly at ~10x earnings with ~22% ROCE — genuinely cheap, but revenue growth is modest, ROCE is drifting down, and Q4 profit was distorted by a prior-year tax item; working capital is dragging returns. **Watch** — attractive value but growth engine unproven; needs revenue re-acceleration.
- **Salzer Electronics (₹1,030 cr):** Scale in switchgear/cables (FY26 revenue ₹1,758 cr) but **ROCE ~11%, ROE ~9%, 17.2% promoter pledge, negative FY25 CFO**, and insider-trading code violations reported Mar 2026. **Reject on quality + governance.**
- **Kernex Microsystems / Quadrant Future Tek:** Kavach-adjacent but loss-making / operating compression in FY26 (Quadrant FY26 PAT ~ –₹43 cr) — too speculative. **Reject on fundamentals.**
- **Genus Power:** see Part C — **Watch, governance-gated.**

### How the funnel resolved
Of the sectors screened, transformers/T&D and railway-safety indigenisation produced the highest concentration of names where all three lenses (SMILE, Lynch GARP, Buffett quality) agree. **Shilchar** is the standout because it uniquely combines still-low institutional ownership (fresh NSE listing), elite debt-free returns, a credible self-funded expansion into a huge durable market, and a valuation that — on a 12% hurdle — implies growth it can plausibly beat. **HBL** matches on quality but has outgrown the neglect criterion and needs to prove post-Q4 execution. The remaining names are either investability-constrained (Danish Power's SME listing), alignment-constrained (Shivalik's 33% promoter holding), governance-constrained (Genus's ED probe), or valuation-constrained (Azad, Avalon).

*This report is a research starting point for further due diligence, not investment advice. Every figure should be independently re-verified against primary BSE/NSE filings, annual reports and concall transcripts before any capital is committed. Micro- and small-cap stocks carry high volatility and liquidity risk, and prices/ownership data move continuously — the market caps and ratios here are dated to mid-2026 and will drift. Position sizing and diversification are essential in this segment.*
