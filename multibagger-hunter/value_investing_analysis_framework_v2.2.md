# Forensic Financial Analysis & Value/GARP Investing Framework (v2.2)

Act as a forensic financial analyst and an expert value/GARP investor deeply steeped in the core tenets of Warren Buffett, Charlie Munger, Benjamin Graham, Peter Lynch, and Vijay Kedia. Your goal is to dissect the provided company with extreme skepticism, actively trying to **invalidate** the investment thesis rather than confirm it — while not throwing out a genuine long-runway compounder on a valuation technicality.

Conduct a rigorous fundamental, operational, and valuation analysis on: **[INSERT COMPANY NAME & TICKER HERE]**, at a current share price of **[INSERT CURRENT SHARE PRICE]**.

If a position is already held, state **[SHARES HELD & AVERAGE COST]** — but see the cost-basis rule in the Exit section: the average cost is an input for sizing and tax only, **never** an input to the verdict.

## How to weigh the evidence — READ BEFORE YOU START (the hierarchy of judgement)

A multibagger is identified by the **business**, not by a discounted-cash-flow spreadsheet. Keep two categories of evidence strictly separate:

- **HARD GATES — these, and only these, can kill the thesis on their own:**
  1. **Business quality** — durable high growth, a large runway, real cash-backed profits, and high, sustainable returns on capital (Sections 1, 5, 6).
  2. **Governance & forensic integrity** — honest, aligned management; clean accounting; no tunneling; cash that matches reported profit (Sections 2, 3).
  A clear failure at either gate ends the analysis → **Avoid / Exit**, regardless of how cheap the stock looks.

- **LENSES — these set the price you pay and the size you take, but never veto an A+ business:**
  - **Valuation (Section 4)** exists to stop you overpaying at euphoria and to size your margin of safety and re-rating headroom. The most a DCF can do *on its own* is move a name from **Buy to Wait/Watch** (pay less, or wait for a better entry). It cannot, by itself, disqualify a business that has cleared both hard gates.

- **MANDATE FIT — a third, separate axis (Section 0).** Distinct from both gates and lenses. A business can be sound, fairly priced, and *still* not belong in this portfolio because its size, liquidity, or return ceiling is incompatible with the mandate. This produces its own verdict (**RETIRE**), not a Hold and not an Avoid. Do not let a mandate mismatch masquerade as a quality judgement, and do not let sound quality override a mandate mismatch.

- **TWO SLEEVES — the mandate is not monolithic (Section 0.1a).** This portfolio may run a **Core GARP/multibagger sleeve** (the primary mandate: small, undiscovered, explosive-runway names sized by conviction) and, *optionally and capped*, a **Satellite Quality-Compounder sleeve** for A-grade cash cows caught at a genuine discount. A name that clears both hard gates but fails the *core* mandate purely on its **growth ceiling / size / spent discovery** is not automatically waste — if, and only if, it is *also on sale*, it routes to the satellite test (new **OWN — Quality Compounder** verdict, Section 8), not straight to reject. This lane is deliberately narrow and gated (a discount is mandatory; fair value is still a Hold/Retire) so it cannot become a dumping ground for "couldn't find a multibagger, settled for mediocre." **It is size-agnostic on the upside** — the designated home for *proven* mid- and large-cap businesses too big or too discovered for the core sleeve — and it holds **two flavours that clear the identical gates:** a quality **compounder** at a discount, and a **proven business at a deep-value / re-rating discount** offering ~2–3x on a wide margin of safety. The satellite sleeve is a portfolio-construction choice the investor turns on explicitly and sizes themselves; if they run a pure single-mandate book, this lane collapses back to RETIRE.

- **Weight the two valuation tools unequally.** **Reverse DCF** is the primary lens — it is an *expectations diagnostic* ("what does today's price require to be true?"), which is falsifiable. **Forward DCF** is a *light sanity check only* — it manufactures a value from far-out cash flows it cannot really forecast and is structurally blind to the re-rating engine (a discovery/sentiment event, not a cash-flow event). **Every forward-DCF output is a wide directional range, never a single-point target price.**

- **What actually breaks a multibagger thesis:** (a) overpaying at a hype/cyclical top — the reverse DCF guards this; and (b) the story breaking — fraud, working-capital trap, serial dilution, competitive erosion — and the **forensic and quality gates** guard that, *not* the DCF.

## 0. Mandate & Applicability Screen — RUN THIS FIRST, BEFORE ANY OTHER WORK

Two cheap checks that prevent expensive mistakes. Complete both before Section 1. If either fails, say so at the top of the output — do not bury it in Section 8.

### 0.1 Mandate Fit Screen

State the mandate parameters being applied, then score the name against them:

| Criterion | Mandate requirement | This company | Pass / Fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market cap | [state the band, e.g. Rs 300-2,500 cr] | | |
| Institutional discovery potential | Pre-discovery / underfollowed | FII % + DII % + shareholder count | |
| SMILE "S" - distance from the institutional threshold (~Rs 5,000-6,000 cr) | Below it, with room to grow into it | | |
| Sell-side coverage | Sparse to none | | |
| Plausible return ceiling (from Section 7 napkin math) | Consistent with the mandate's target multiple | | |

**Tiered size band (market cap is a proxy; ownership is the substance).** The single rupee number is a cliff only if you make it one. Every size test is really proxying one question — *"has the institutional-discovery re-rating leg already happened?"* Run three tiers, and let the ownership/coverage evidence override the rupee figure wherever they disagree:
- **Core sweet spot (template ~₹300–2,500 cr):** full core mandate; both engines (earnings growth + discovery re-rating) live.
- **Extended / graduation-watch (template ~₹2,500–5,000 cr):** still *eligible for the core sleeve*, but **only** if the ownership data confirms it is **genuinely still undiscovered** (low FII+DII, sparse/no sell-side coverage, not in an index) **and** the Section 7 napkin math shows the **earnings engine carries most of the return** — because the re-rating headroom to the ~₹5–6k institutional threshold is now thin, and you can no longer lean on the discovery leg. A name in this band that is *already* fund-owned fails here regardless of the rupee figure.
- **Hard ceiling (~₹5–6k cr — the institutional threshold — and above):** discovery is *presumed spent* → the core mandate fails → route to RETIRE or the satellite test, **unless** the ownership data actively proves institutions still haven't entered (rare, but when true it decides it).

**Where market cap and ownership disagree, ownership wins:** a ₹4,000 cr name that is 3% institution-owned and uncovered is more genuinely pre-discovery than a ₹1,500 cr name already 25% held by funds. The rupee band is only a fast first filter. These numbers are templates the investor calibrates — market-cap inflation and rising domestic (SIP/DII) flows push the practical discovery threshold up over time, so revisit them periodically rather than treating them as fixed.

**Graduation-zone rule.** A name that has *already passed through* the pre-discovery phase is a mandate disqualifier **independent of business quality**. Institutional ownership above ~20%, saturated sell-side coverage, or index membership means the discovery re-rating leg this framework is built to exploit **no longer exists**. Say this explicitly and in the first paragraph of the output.

**Satellite routing (do this instead of a straight reject).** A core-mandate fail is not automatically waste. Classify *why* it failed:
- Failed on **quality or governance** (a hard gate) → reject: **AVOID / EXIT**. The satellite lane is closed to it.
- Failed **only** on the growth ceiling / size / spent-discovery axis, with **both hard gates clean and a real per-share compounding record** → it is a candidate for the **Satellite Quality-Compounder test (Section 8)**. But it earns an **OWN — Quality Compounder** verdict *only if it is also trading at a genuine discount* (margin of safety, per the satellite gate in Section 8). At fair value it is **HOLD** (if owned) or **RETIRE/pass** (if not) — never a satellite Buy. **A discount is the entire difference between "quality cash cow worth buying into a satellite sleeve" and "fine business, nothing to do here."**

**Do not proceed to a full eight-section analysis on a name that is 10x+ outside the mandate band unless the user has explicitly asked for it anyway.** If they have, run the analysis but carry the mandate verdict forward to Section 8 and expect **RETIRE**, not Hold.

### 0.2 Sector Applicability Adapter — does the framework's machinery actually work on this business?

Much of this framework assumes an industrial/consumer business with inventory, receivables, capex, and free cash flow. On **banks, NBFCs, insurers, holding companies, REITs/InvITs, and asset-heavy financial intermediaries** several core tools produce *actively misleading* output. Identify the business type and, where required, substitute:

| Framework tool | Breaks on | Why | Mandatory substitute |
|---|---|---|---|
| FCF / Owners' Earnings DCF | Banks, NBFCs, insurers | Deposits/borrowings and loan growth flow *through* operating cash flow; CFO becomes balance-sheet noise (can swing from large negative to large positive with no operating meaning) | **Two-stage residual income (excess return) model** + **justified P/B = (ROE-g)/(CoE-g)** + DDM sanity check. For insurers: **P/EV** |
| CFO/PAT conversion, cash-conversion cycle, working-capital-days, "working capital trap" gate | Banks, NBFCs, insurers | No inventory, no trade receivables in the industrial sense | **Asset-quality integrity gate:** GNPA, NNPA as % of net worth, PCR, slippage ratio, SMA-1/SMA-2 book, restructured book, CET1/CRAR |
| ROCE | Any lender | Interest expense is cost of goods, not financing — ROCE is arithmetically meaningless | **RoA and ROE, reported together.** Never quote ROE without the leverage multiple beside it |
| Screener's auto-generated "Free Cash Flow", "CFO/OP %", "Working capital days", "might be capitalizing interest cost", "contingent liabilities" flag | Banks, NBFCs, insurers | All machine-generated on industrial assumptions; contingent liabilities for a bank are LCs, guarantees and derivative notionals — normal course of business | **Disregard entirely and say so**, so the reader knows it was considered and rejected, not overlooked |
| Operating-leverage inflection / the RJ setup (Section 5) | Banks, NBFCs, insurers, most asset-light services | No fixed-cost/contribution-margin breakeven structure; growth requires **proportional** regulatory capital, which is the definitional opposite of operating leverage | **Cost-to-income ratio + credit-cost normalisation.** State plainly that the RJ setup is structurally absent rather than forcing the diagnostic |
| Depreciation / maintenance capex / CWIP analysis | Financials, asset-light services | Immaterial to the economics | Skip, with a one-line note |
| Standard DCF terminal value | Holding companies | Value is the sum of stakes, not a cash-flow stream | **Sum-of-the-parts with an explicit, justified holding-company discount** |

**Rule: if you find yourself substituting more than three tools, say in the output that the framework is being run in adapted form and name every substitution.** A reader must never be unable to tell which conclusions came from the framework as designed and which came from a workaround.

**Financials — additional MANDATORY checks the substitutions above do not cover.** The adapter table fixes the *tools*; these fix the *analysis*. For any bank / NBFC / HFC / insurer, run all of these or state why one is N/A:

1. **The moat lives on the LIABILITY side, not the asset side (banks).** A bank's durable competitive advantage is a **low-cost, sticky, granular deposit franchise** — measure it: **CASA ratio, cost of funds vs. peers, deposit granularity (retail vs. bulk), and their trend.** Anyone can grow a loan book by pricing aggressively; almost no one can build a cheap deposit base. A bank whose asset quality looks fine but whose funding is expensive/bulk/wholesale has *no moat* and is one liquidity event from trouble. Score the deposit franchise as the Section 1 moat; a high-NIM book funded by hot money is not quality.
2. **NBFCs/HFCs have NO deposit franchise — their liability quality is a distinct existential risk.** They borrow short (banks/CPs/NCDs) and lend long. The failure mode is a **funding freeze** (IL&FS, DHFL). Test: **ALM gaps (short-tenor liabilities vs. long-tenor assets), borrowing-mix concentration, share of short-term/CP funding, and refinancing/rollover dependence.** A fast-growing NBFC reliant on wholesale short-term funding is running a maturity-mismatch risk that no ROE can compensate for. Do not lump NBFCs with banks on the liability side.
3. **NIM-cycle normalisation (the double-peak trap).** Net interest margin is a spread that moves with the rate cycle. A lender printing **peak NIM *and* trough credit cost simultaneously is double-peaked** — its "cheap" trailing P/E is the market correctly refusing to capitalise unsustainable earnings (this is the financials version of Section 5's cycle-extreme reverse check). Normalise NIM to mid-cycle and re-run; if the P/E is only cheap on peak-NIM/trough-provision earnings, it is a value trap, not a discount.
4. **Internal capital generation vs. dilution-dependence (the growth-funding test — critical for the mandate).** A lender's *self-funding* growth rate ≈ **ROE × retention ratio**. If targeted loan/AUM growth exceeds that, the balance sheet **cannot** support it internally — equity raises are coming. Model them: **a growth-hungry lender raising equity *below book* is per-share value destruction** (tie this to Section 2's dilution taxonomy). This is often the real ceiling on a "high-growth" financial's per-share compounding, and it is invisible on a P/E screen. State whether growth is self-funding or dilution-dependent, and at what price any raise would be accretive vs. dilutive.
5. **Cost of Equity (CoE) discipline for justified P/B.** Justified P/B = (ROE − g)/(CoE − g) is **hypersensitive to CoE and explodes as g → CoE** (already flagged as model-fragility). Use the framework's personal-hurdle discipline for CoE — name it (typically **~12–14% for Indian financials**), do **not** re-derive it from the risk-free rate each quarter, **show a P/B sensitivity grid across CoE and g, and never present a single-point P/B target.** Anchor on a justified-multiple *range* plus a peer cross-check on comparable ROE, not the fragile point estimate.
6. **Insurers — the right lens is embedded value.** Value on **P/EV**, and check **VNB margin, VNB growth, and persistency (13th/61st month)**; distinguish life (EV/VNB) from general/health (combined ratio, P/B on float quality). Persistency deterioration is the insurer's version of a leaking moat.

**Reported book can be fiction — haircut it.** For any lender, the reported net worth is only as good as the provisioning behind it. Cross-check **NNPA as a % of net worth**, PCR trend, the restructured/SMA-1/SMA-2 book, write-offs, and loans sold to ARCs. Under-provisioning flatters both PAT *and* book — which corrupts the residual-income model *and* the justified-P/B numerator at the same time. State whether reported book needs a haircut before you value it.

### 0.3 Data-Source Integrity Pre-Check (recurring failure modes — check all five)

1. **Live price.** Screener's cached price is frequently stale. Cross-check against a live quote *before* any valuation work; note the date and time of both. Stale prices have materially changed verdicts.
2. **Split/bonus adjustment.** Test the reported historical EPS series against `PAT / (equity capital / face value)` for the earliest year available. If they don't reconcile, the EPS series is partially unadjusted — **do not use it for long-run per-share CAGRs. Recompute from equity capital and net worth.**
3. **Consolidation discontinuities.** Check whether any subsidiary was acquired, deconsolidated, listed, or dropped below 50% during the period. A subsidiary falling below 50% becomes an associate, and revenue/expense lines break discontinuously — making YoY comparisons invalid. **Name the quarter and the cause.**
4. **PAT vs. EPS reconciliation.** Confirm `reported EPS x share count ~ reported PAT`. A gap means minority interest, exceptional items, or a data error. If consolidated figures don't reconcile, **use standalone and say why.**
5. **Third-party feed errors.** Order-of-magnitude errors (million reported as crore), consolidated group metrics conflated with standalone listed-entity metrics, and 10x revenue errors all circulate. Audited filings take precedence over every aggregator.
6. **Base-year integrity for EVERY growth CAGR (the low-base trap — mandatory).** Before quoting *any* multi-year CAGR — revenue, EBITDA, PAT, EPS — inspect the **base year** of the window. If the base is a distorted trough (a COVID year, a one-off loss/impairment year, a near-zero-denominator year, a strike/plant-shutdown year, a pre-turnaround crisis year), the CAGR is an artefact of the recovery, **not** evidence of sustainable growth, and must be **discarded or recomputed off a normalised base.** Concretely: a "132% 5-yr PAT CAGR" built off a ₹2 cr COVID-crushed base year is meaningless; restate it off the pre-distortion year (e.g. FY20) and report the honest normalised figure (~27%). Screener/aggregator headline CAGRs are the usual source of this error. **Rule: if a trailing CAGR uses a sub-normal base year, do not carry it into the growth input for Sections 4, 5, or 7 — flag it, normalise it, and use the normalised number.** State the base year you used and why.
7. **Live-events & recency search (mandatory before finalising the governance and risk gates).** Screener and annual reports are backward-looking; the fact that moves the verdict is often three weeks old. Search the last ~6 months for: pending litigation or Supreme Court/High Court/tribunal matters, SEBI/RBI/regulatory action, **lease/licence/concession renewal disputes**, credit-rating changes with rationale, promoter pledge/stake changes, auditor or board resignations, and any threat to a core asset's *tenure or operating licence*. State the date of the most recent material item found. For a **single-asset or single-licence business, a live threat to that asset's tenure/licence is an existential (hard-gate) risk, not a peripheral one** — it belongs in Section 3's top risks and can cap conviction regardless of how clean the financials look. If the search surfaces nothing, say so explicitly ("no live adverse events found as of [date]") — silence is not the same as having checked.

## 1. Business Model, Moat & Market Dynamics  *(HARD GATE — business quality)*
- **The Business Blueprint:** What exactly does this business do, and how does it convert operations into actual liquid cash? Who pays them, and what triggers recurring revenue? Give a concise overview of primary revenue segments (% mix, growth, margin profile), customer concentration, and geographic exposure.
- **The Moat Assessment:** Is there a durable competitive advantage (switching costs, network effects, structural cost leadership, brand/intangibles)? Or is it a commodity price-taker exposed to intense competition? Can it reliably earn **Return on Incremental Invested Capital (ROIIC) > cost of capital** over the next decade? Note whether the moat is **widening or eroding — and prove the direction with a multi-period metric trail** (pricing/realisation, gross or net margin, spread, market share) rather than asserting it. Where pricing is **set or auto-repriced by regulation, contract, or benchmark linkage**, say so explicitly: that is price-taking by structure, not by competitive weakness, and it is not fixable by management.
- **Sector Outlook, Headwinds & Demand Integrity:** Macro trajectory over 5–10 years; is the industry fragmenting or consolidating? **Crucially, judge demand integrity:** is recent underperformance an operational hiccup (temporary margin compression, inventory destocking, transitory regulation) or a *structural* decline (product obsolescence, permanent moat loss, changed consumer behaviour)? State unequivocally: business to avoid, or temporary hiccup to bet on.
- **Capacity & the Growth Ceiling (required — the "industry is growing" fallacy).** State the company's **current capacity utilisation** and the **physical/structural ceiling on volume**: installed capacity vs. output, occupancy, licence/quota limits, land or footprint constraints, deposit/funding constraints for a lender, billable-headcount limits for a services firm. **A capacity-bound business cannot grow volumes with its industry — it grows with its *own* beds/tonnes/branches/seats until named new capacity commissions.** Industry demand growth (TAM growth) is therefore *irrelevant* as the company's growth rate wherever the company is capacity-constrained; using it is the single most common way an analyst overstates achievable growth. If growth beyond the current ceiling requires new capacity, **name the project, its cost, its commissioning date, and the lag before it contributes** — and treat everything before that date as ceiling-limited (price/mix only). This number is the hard input to Section 4's achievable-growth comparison; carry it forward.
- **Per-Share Primacy (required).** A compounder compounds **per share**. Restate the full available history to today's share count and report:
  - **Book value per share** and **EPS** at the start and end of the longest available window, with the CAGR of each
  - **Net worth growth multiple vs. share count growth multiple** side by side
  - **Segment the history by capital-structure regime.** Do not report a single long CAGR across a period containing a heavy dilution episode — it conflates two different businesses. Report the crisis/dilution era and the flat-share-count era **separately**, e.g. *"BVPS -5% FY15-FY26 overall, but that is entirely FY15-FY22 recapitalisation dilution; share count has been flat since FY22 and BVPS has compounded 13.9% over those four years."* The long-window number is a **governance warning about crisis behaviour**; the recent-regime number is the **evidence about today's engine.** Both matter, for different questions. Reporting only one is a serious analytical error in either direction.
  - **Hard-gate call:** a decade of flat or negative BVPS/EPS per share is a business-quality failure *for a compounder mandate*, even where the enterprise grew. State whether the failure is "not a compounder" or "not a viable business" — **these are different verdicts** (see Section 8, Step 1).

## 2. Management, Capital Allocation & Anti-Tunneling Checks  *(HARD GATE — governance)*
- **Minority Shareholder Treatment & Governance:** Any history of management favouring themselves over minorities? Red flags: high promoter remuneration, excessive related-party transactions, renting property from promoters, opaque multi-layer subsidiary structures. Direct review of annual-report RPT schedules is required — screener pages and third-party sources routinely omit or misrepresent these.
- **Controlling-Shareholder Taxonomy (required — do NOT score promoter holding generically).** "High promoter holding, zero pledge" is only a positive signal for a *founder/family owner-operator*. Classify the promoter and score accordingly:

  | Promoter type | How to read high holding | How to read zero pledge | Specific risk to test |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **Founder / family owner-operator** | Genuine skin in the game — **positive** | Genuine positive | Succession; RPT tunneling; key-man |
  | **Sovereign / State (PSU)** | **Not** alignment — a policy actor. Positive only for solvency backstop | **Meaningless** — a sovereign cannot pledge. Do not credit it | **Below-book recapitalisation diluting minorities**; directed/priority lending; policy delivery burden; fixed short CEO terms (typically ~3 years) precluding owner-operator alignment |
  | **Professionally managed / institutional / MNC parent** | Neutral | Neutral | Parent-subsidiary transfer pricing; royalty extraction; minority squeeze-out |
  | **PE / financial sponsor** | **Negative-leaning** — exit-driven | Neutral | Overhang from block sales; leverage loaded pre-exit |

  Where the promoter is sovereign or a financial sponsor, **the historical dilution record is the governance test that matters**, not the pledge column.
- **Capex Quality & Capital Allocation — the $1 Retained-Earnings Test:** Review historical ROCE and ROE. Is capex generating high incremental returns, or is it value-destroying empire-building? **Apply Buffett's $1 Test: for every rupee of earnings retained, has the company created at least a rupee of market value? Show the math and state whether retention is creating or destroying shareholder wealth.**
  - **Run the test over TWO windows and report both: (a) the most recent 3–5 years, and (b) the longest available window that INCLUDES the worst drawdown or dilution episode.** A 5-year test starting from a distressed trough measures the re-rating, not the retention quality, and will pass almost any recovering cyclical. Where the two windows disagree, **the full-cycle window is the one that governs the hard gate**; the recent window describes the current regime.
  - Also decompose net-worth growth: `retained earnings vs. fresh equity injected`. State **what % of net-worth growth came from new shares rather than earnings.** Above ~25% from new shares over a cycle is a serious per-share warning. *(When reviewing trailing ROCE/ROE, first distinguish a temporary capex-cycle / commissioning trough from structural erosion — see Section 5, step 3 — and do **not** penalise a genuine inflection on depressed trailing returns alone; judge the trajectory, not just the last print.)*
- **Reinvestment Runway vs. Return of Capital:** Separate two distinct questions. (a) Does the business earn high returns on *incremental* capital (ROIIC), and (b) does it have a genuine runway to *redeploy* its cash at those same high rates? The ideal is high returns **and** a long reinvestment runway (a compounding machine). But if it throws off cash it **cannot** reinvest at high rates, the correct move is to **return capital** — rising dividends and/or buybacks *executed at sensible prices* — **not** to hoard idle cash or "diworsify" into low-return, non-core ventures to look busy. Judge which of these three the company is: (i) high-return + long runway (reinvest — best), (ii) high-return + no runway but disciplined payout (acceptable cash cow), or (iii) high-return + no runway + empire-building/idle cash (capital-allocation red flag). State explicitly which one this is. **Add a fourth case where it applies: (iv) *moderate*-return + long runway — reinvesting at only 1–2 points above cost of capital is technically accretive and practically unexciting; say so plainly rather than scoring it as a compounder.**
- **M&A / Inorganic-Growth Forensics:** Has the company made mergers or acquisitions? For each material deal, assess: **(1) Moat** — did the acquired business carry a durable competitive advantage that *protects or widens* the parent's profit pool, or was it an unrelated bolt-on bought for size? **(2) Accretion vs. dilution** — did the deal actually raise the *per-share* value existing owners hold? Check whether EPS **and BVPS** rose after the deal (earnings % grew faster than share-count %), i.e. owners ended up with a *smaller slice of a much bigger, more valuable pie* rather than a bigger slice of a smaller one; quantify the change in an existing shareholder's per-share earnings and value, not just absolute company size. **(3) Financing discipline** — if equity was raised to fund it, was that raise *actually necessary*? Was there sufficient cash / balance-sheet capacity to fund it internally? If they diluted shareholders while sitting on deployable cash (or cheap debt capacity), that is dilution for little reason and a management-quality demerit. **(4) Whose decision was it?** Distinguish management-initiated M&A from state-directed or regulator-mandated amalgamation — the per-share outcome is judged the same way, but the *management-quality inference* differs entirely. Conclude whether each deal created or destroyed per-share value for existing owners.
  - **Credit non-dilutive monetisation where it occurs.** Subsidiary IPOs/stake sales structured as pure OFS bring in cash without issuing parent shares. Quantify the realisation against the carrying/acquisition cost and score it as the capital-allocation positive it is. **Always distinguish an OFS by the promoter (non-dilutive to you) from a fresh issue (dilutive).** Conflating the two is a common and costly error.
- **Visionary Leadership & Execution Credibility:** Is management aligned, credible, and able to scale? Does historical guidance match actual delivery? Build a **guidance-vs-delivery table** across at least four data points and separate the categories: management may be reliable on volume and asset quality while chronically over-optimistic on price/margin. Where the misses cluster on a variable management does not control, that is evidence for Section 1's moat conclusion, not merely a forecasting demerit. Strategic foresight, or merely riding tailwinds?
- **Corporate Alignment:** Promoter/insider holding %; any **pledged** shares; recent board/executive shake-ups; promoter holding stability over 8+ years. *(Read all of this through the taxonomy above, not generically — high unpledged holding is only unambiguously positive for a founder/family owner-operator.)*

## 3. Forensic Cash Flow & Operational Risks  *(HARD GATE — governance/quality)*
- **Accounting Profit vs. Cash Reality:** Is there a divergence between PAT and Cash Flow from Operations? Track the cash-conversion cycle (Debtor + Inventory vs. Payable days) over 3 years. Is cash trapped in "unbilled revenue" or "retention money"? Cumulative CFO/PAT conversion over 5–7 years is more diagnostic than any single year. Many micro-caps are working-capital value traps — state explicitly whether this one fits that profile. Genuine commissioning troughs (CWIP-driven) are distinguishable from structural working-capital traps by the composition of the drag (inventory-led vs. debtor-led).
  - **For lenders/insurers, substitute the asset-quality integrity gate per Section 0.2** and report NNPA as a % of net worth (the number that tells you whether reported book needs a haircut), alongside PCR and CET1.
- **Earnings-Quality Direction Check (required).** Establish whether profit growth over the last 3 years came from **operating expansion** (volume x price x mix, with margins up) or from a **declining cost/provision line** (falling credit costs, falling raw-material costs, one-off write-backs, a depressed base, other income). A profit ramp driven by a cost line that is now at a **cycle floor** is not an earnings engine — it is a spent tailwind, and it will reverse. Name which it is. Where operating profit is falling while reported PAT rises, say so: that divergence is the single clearest signal that the tailwind is exhausted.
- **Top Structural Risks & the Imperfect-to-Perfect Path:** Top 3–4 structural/existential risks (client concentration, regulatory shifts, debt burden, raw-material margin vulnerability). **Concurrently** — is this an "imperfect" business (legacy capital-allocation mistakes, an ugly unsegmented unit, temporary bad press) with a clear, visible path to optimization over 24–36 months? **The imperfect-to-perfect setup requires the fix to be AHEAD of you.** If the clean-up already happened — asset quality repaired, margins already normalised, multiple already re-rated off the distressed base — you are being offered the position *after* the repair. Say so; it is the opposite of the setup, not a version of it. Quantify each named risk as an EPS or book-value sensitivity where possible. **Pull the live legal/regulatory/lease/licence items from the Section 0.3 recency search into this list with their dates.** For a single-asset, single-lease, or single-licence business, a live threat to that asset's *tenure, licence, or concession* is an existential risk that can independently cap the verdict — do not file it under "peripheral overhang." Where the outcome is binary and out of management's hands (a pending court ruling, a lease non-renewal, a licence cancellation), model it as an explicit **bear scenario with a probability and a rupee impact**, not a footnote.

## 4. Valuation — Reverse & Forward DCF  *(LENS, not a gate)*
Baseline metric rule: **do NOT use standard FCF if the company is in a hyper-growth phase** (growth capex would unduly penalise it). Instead use **Owners' Earnings** (Net Income + D&A − *maintenance* capex) or **normalized operating cash flow before growth capex**. **For financials, use the Section 0.2 substitutes — residual income and justified P/B — and say so.**

**Discount-rate policy.** Use a **constant personal base hurdle of 10–12%** — do NOT re-derive it from wherever the risk-free rate sits this quarter; the point is a stable yardstick. Reserve **15% for businesses that are genuinely higher-risk on their own merits** (weak/narrowing moat, high cyclicality, heavy customer/geography concentration, unproven execution, opaque model) — **not** as a reflex simply because the company is a micro-cap or SME. Run and show **10%, 12% and 15%** side by side for sensitivity, then **recommend the single rate that best fits THIS business and justify it from the business's actual risk profile.** *Intermediate rates (e.g. 13–14%) are permitted where the business genuinely sits between the bands — but you must justify the choice from named business characteristics, list what argues for a higher rate and what argues for a lower one, and still show the three mandated rates for comparability.* Add to the 15%-warranting list: **extreme balance-sheet leverage, and a controlling shareholder with a dilution record.**

- **Reverse DCF (primary lens) — what is the market baking in?** Back out the 10-year growth CAGR the current price implies at each of the three rates. *(For financials: back out the **implied sustainable ROE** instead — the analogous falsifiable question.)* Compare it to what the business can *realistically* achieve, **quantified from TAM × plausible market share** and the industry's secular tailwinds — not asserted. For financials, quantify achievable from guided RoA × leverage, and from consensus/management RoA and ROE targets.
  - **Cap the achievable number at the Section 1 growth ceiling (mandatory).** The "realistically achievable" growth you compare implied growth against is the **company's** achievable growth given its capacity — **not** the industry's demand growth. If the business is capacity-constrained (Section 1), achievable volume growth is the **price/mix component only** until dated new capacity commissions; do not plug industry/TAM growth into a bed-, tonne-, branch-, or seat-limited company. When new capacity is on the way, model it as a *step* from its commissioning date with a ramp, not as a smooth decade-long rate. **A verdict that only works because the company was assumed to grow at the industry's rate, when it physically cannot, is the specific error this clause exists to stop** — it is what turned one prior IMCL run into a false Buy (13% "base case" = industry demand growth on a hospital that couldn't add beds until FY28).
  - Implied growth **modest and clearly beatable** → the gap is your margin of safety *and* re-rating fuel → lean **Buy**.
  - Implied growth **≥ what's plausibly achievable** → the re-rating is largely spent → move toward **Wait/Watch** (you'd be relying on the earnings engine alone, with no re-rating cushion and full downside on a stumble).
  - **Asymmetry — read carefully.** Reverse DCF is *reliable at pulling you back from euphoric names* and *unreliable at the false reject*. The graveyard of missed compounders is full of people who reverse-DCF'd a great business, said "priced for perfection, pass," and missed a 10–20-bagger. A **high** implied-growth number on a genuine hypergrowth machine in a huge, durable market is often *achievable*, not a red flag — do **not** auto-reject it. Reverse DCF may push a name to Watch; it may not, by itself, kill an A+ business.
  - **Asymmetry-applicability test (required).** Before invoking the false-reject warning as a reason to be lenient, confirm it actually applies. The warning protects businesses with **expanding returns and an undiscovered runway**. It does **not** protect a business with contracting margins, a flat or negative per-share record, and a fully discovered shareholder register. State which case you are in — otherwise the asymmetry clause becomes a universal excuse.

- **Forward DCF matrix (sanity check only).** Project **Bear / Base / Bull** growth over a 10-year high-growth phase into a conservative ~5% terminal rate, grounded in industry dynamics, competitive intensity, and the company's own execution history. Compute intrinsic value across **all three discount rates** → an intrinsic-value **RANGE, never a single point**. Flag how sensitive the output is to the terminal assumption.
  - **Model-fragility disclosure (required).** Where the model's output explodes as growth approaches the discount rate (endemic to residual-income and DDM models on financials), demonstrate the fragility with a small sensitivity grid and then **name which of your tools is the most robust anchor** — usually a justified-multiple table plus a peer cross-check on comparable returns. Do not present a fragile point estimate as though it were the finding.

- **Double-counting flag (required).** A higher discount rate mechanically raises the growth number a reverse DCF says the price "requires." So if you both (i) apply 15% *and* (ii) penalise the name for "implied growth looks too high," you are charging for risk **twice** — once in the rate, once in the verdict. **Whenever you use 15% (or any rate above the 10–12% base), state plainly whether the verdict is partly double-counting risk, and re-check the call at 10–12%.** Say which rate you ultimately stand behind and why. **Then state whether the verdict is stable across the rate range or flips with it** — a conclusion that only holds at one rate is a conclusion about the rate, not about the business, and must be disclosed as such.

## 5. Re-Rating, Operating-Leverage Inflection & Catalyst
- **The Valuation Trap:** If the company hits its growth but the market re-rates the multiple *down*, what's the capital-loss risk? (e.g., 25% earnings grower de-rating from 60x to a 20x industry mean.) Map the structural downside in rupees. **Also identify which of the two risks dominates:** multiple contraction (typical of high-multiple growth names) or **earnings contraction** (typical of low-multiple cyclicals, where the multiple can barely de-rate further but the earnings base can halve). These require different monitoring and different triggers.
- **Optical Illusion & Hidden Operating Leverage — show the numbers.** Do not assert "expensive on TTM but cheap on forward" — **prove it with a small table.** Lay out: current price; **TTM EPS and TTM P/E**; projected **FY+1 and FY+2 EPS** (state the growth assumptions and what drives them — capacity commissioning / capex→opex, a normalising depressed base, an order book converting, operating leverage); and the resulting **implied forward P/E for FY+1 and FY+2**. Then read it off explicitly, e.g. *"₹500 price ÷ ₹10 TTM EPS = 50x TTM; if EPS ramps to ₹18 (FY+1) and ₹28 (FY+2), the forward P/E compresses to ~28x then ~18x — the multiple 'de-rates' purely through earnings even if the price is flat."* 
  - **Distinguish non-linear compression from linear compression.** A 50x -> 28x -> 18x collapse signals stored leverage. A 6.0x -> 5.5x -> 5.1x drift off a single-digit base is ordinary earnings growth and signals nothing — do not present it as an optical illusion.
  - **Do the REVERSE check with numbers, and name the cycle-extreme input.** Is it optically cheap on TTM but sitting on **peak/cyclical-top EPS** about to mean-revert, so the *forward* P/E is actually higher (a value trap)? To do this properly, **identify explicitly which input is at a cycle extreme** — credit cost, commodity spread, freight rate, realisation, utilisation, provision write-back — then **re-run the multiple across a range from the current extreme to a normalised mid-cycle level**, and read off what happens to both the P/E and the justified multiple. A low multiple on unsustainable earnings is not a discount; it is the market correctly pricing earnings it does not believe.
- **Operating-Leverage Inflection — the "profits about to explode" diagnostic (the RJ setup).** The single most powerful micro-cap setup: a business whose *fixed cost base is already built and paid for*, whose revenue has just crossed — or is about to cross — its fixed-cost breakeven, so that **each incremental rupee of revenue falls almost entirely to EBIT**, expanding margins and profits *non-linearly*. Depressed current margins keep the crowd away (it screens "expensive" on trailing earnings), which is exactly why it's cheap. Diagnose it with numbers, not adjectives:
  1. **Cost structure & contribution margin.** Split costs into fixed vs. variable; compute contribution margin = (revenue − variable cost) / revenue. High fixed-cost + high contribution-margin businesses have the most explosive leverage — model it.
  2. **Breakeven position.** Where does current revenue sit relative to the fixed-cost breakeven? The sweet spot is revenue *at or just past* breakeven — beyond that point, incremental revenue at the contribution margin flows overwhelmingly to EBIT. **If the business is already at or above its own historical PEAK profitability, the setup is absent — say so and stop.** The diagnostic looks for depressed-and-about-to-inflect, not strong-and-about-to-normalise.
  3. **Why margins *and returns* are depressed (the temporary vs. structural test).** Diagnose the depression in **both** margins **and** the return ratios (ROCE/ROE/ROIC) — and do **not** reject a genuine inflection on trailing returns alone. **Temporary / self-correcting:** a recently commissioned plant moves from CWIP into the asset base, *inflating the capital-employed denominator*, while its depreciation, interest and fixed opex *drag the numerator* and revenue hasn't yet ramped — so ROCE/ROE/ROIC mechanically **trough right after commissioning and then recover** (often to *above* the old level, via operating leverage) as utilisation climbs. Depressed margins from the same under-absorption behave identically. **Structural (reject):** returns eroding *despite* stable/full utilisation, competition compressing pricing, or no capex/commissioning explanation at all. **Demand evidence, not assertion:** to call it temporary you must point to the *specific* plant, its commissioning date, the utilisation ramp, and the order book/demand that will fill it — "it's just temporary" with no named driver is how value traps get rationalised. State plainly which it is, and separate the mechanical (denominator just grew) from the earned (returns actually recovering). **Also check the direction of the metric you are calling depressed:** a metric that is at a decade *high* is not depressed, whatever else is compressed.
  4. **Utilisation runway.** Current capacity utilisation and headroom. Low utilisation on a built-out asset base = a long operating-leverage runway with little incremental capex. **Check the funding side too:** if the input constraint (deposits, working capital, raw-material supply) is growing slower than output, that is a constraint forming, not headroom.
  5. **Quantify the non-linearity.** Take the expected incremental revenue over 2–3 years, apply the contribution margin at flat fixed costs, and show the disproportionate EBIT/PAT jump and margin trajectory — e.g. *"at ~55% contribution margin with fixed costs flat, a 30% revenue rise lifts EBIT ~2x and EBIT margin from 8% → 14%."* **Validate the profit-growth signal is real:** confirm any recent/projected profit jump is **operating** — strip out other income, one-off gains, provision write-backs, and low-base flattery — because a leverage story "confirmed" by non-operating income or a falling cost line is a mirage (cross-reference Section 3's earnings-quality direction check).
  6. **Trigger + guardrails.** Name what drives the top-line ramp (order book, demand, a commissioned plant ramping, distribution expansion). Confirm the fixed costs are genuinely fixed (won't step up with volume) and that **ROE/ROCE rise as revenue scales** — **leverage that needs proportional fresh capital is not true operating leverage.** This is the engine that turns a depressed-margin micro-cap into a non-linear earnings story; it is usually both the optical illusion *and* the re-rating catalyst.
- **Catalyst presence, credibility & the dead-money risk (required).** Cheapness does not re-rate a stock — a **catalyst** does, and in the micro/small-cap space a good, cheap business can languish, ignored, for *years* without one.
  - **Spent-Catalyst Inventory (required table).** Before naming what's ahead, audit what has already been consumed. List every plausible catalyst and mark each **SPENT / LIVE / SPECULATIVE**, with evidence:

    | Catalyst | Status | Evidence |
    |---|---|---|
    | Margin/asset-quality normalisation | | |
    | Multiple re-rating off a distressed base | | |
    | Capacity commissioning / order-book conversion | | |
    | Value unlock (demerger, subsidiary listing, stake sale) | | |
    | Index inclusion | | |
    | Institutional entry / sell-side initiation | | |
    | Debt payoff / deleveraging | | |

    **A name whose catalysts are predominantly SPENT is structurally different from one whose catalysts are LIVE, even at identical valuations.** The verdict must reflect this.
  - Then name the **specific, credible, time-bound catalyst** that forces discovery within a medium-term horizon (~1–3 years): the operating-leverage inflection above, capacity commissioning, order-book conversion, margin normalisation, a debt payoff, a demerger/segment unlock, index inclusion, or sell-side initiation. Assess **how credible** it is and **how soon**, and **whether it is within management's control or exogenous** (a rate cycle, a commodity price, a policy decision). An exogenous catalyst with no timeline is not a catalyst — it is a hope. If you *cannot* identify a credible catalyst with a plausible timeline, say so plainly and treat the name as exposed to **prolonged dead money even if the thesis is "right"** — haircut conviction and expected IRR accordingly, because **time is a cost** (a 3x over 8 years ≈ 15% IRR; the same 3x over 3 years ≈ 44%). Distinguish "no catalyst yet → Watch until one emerges" from "catalyst imminent → Buy."
  - **Two distinct dead-money causes — name which applies.** (a) **Obscurity:** a good cheap business nobody looks at; the mispricing is real and will eventually close. (b) **Correct pricing:** the stock is fully discovered and fairly valued, so there is no mispricing left to close and only fundamental improvement can move it. These look identical on a price chart and demand opposite conclusions. (a) is a reason to wait; (b) is a reason to leave.

## 6. The Multibagger Traits Scanner (The Masters' Playbook)
- **Peter Lynch:** Dull/boring/ignored industry? Low institutional ownership? Insider buying or sensible buybacks? Inventories growing slower than sales; any hidden-asset play? *(Quantify hidden assets as a % of market cap — if under ~10%, say so; it is real but not thesis-changing.)*
- **Vijay Kedia — SMILE:**
  - **S (Small in size):** small enough for non-linear expansion. **Identify the institutional threshold** — current market cap, and the size/liquidity level (typically the ~₹5,000–6,000 cr zone) at which MFs/FIIs can build positions and trigger a structural re-rating. **Confirm the public free float and daily traded value are deep enough that funds can actually enter** — a stock no institution can buy can't be re-rated. **Conversely, note when liquidity is more than adequate *because institutions are already in* — that is a fail, not a pass.** Report the multiple of the threshold, not just pass/fail: 1.2x above it is a graduation-zone judgement call; 20x above it is a category error.
  - **M (Medium in experience):** battle-tested through at least one full down-cycle without complacency.
  - **L (Large in aspiration):** aggressive, credible scaling plans and execution velocity. **Check the direction of guidance:** guidance being *lowered* is evidence against aspiration, whatever the commentary says.
  - **E (Extra-large market potential):** small fish, massive expanding pond; can TAM/SAM absorb a 10x revenue scale-up over a decade? **Test the fish as well as the pond** — a vast TAM is irrelevant if the company's own guided growth rate compounds to only 2–3x over ten years. Do the arithmetic.
- **Buffett & Munger:** Undeniable pricing power? Sustained ROE/ROCE >20% *without* heavy leverage — **always report the leverage multiple next to the ROE**, since a mediocre RoA at high leverage produces a flattering ROE that fails this test? Economic engine driven by high-quality owner earnings, not accounting adjustments or a falling cost line?
- **Score the scanner explicitly** (e.g. "SMILE: 1 clear pass of 4; Buffett/Munger: 0 of 3 economic tests"). A narrative summary lets weak names slide through.
- **Micro-to-Mid-Cap Migration Roadmap:** Quantify the realistic path from micro/small-cap to mid-cap. What operational milestones (revenue run-rate, order-book execution, capacity utilisation) must materialise? What could permanently halt the transition (governance upgrades, management professionalization, main-board move, working-capital availability)? **If the company is already mid or large cap, state that the roadmap is not applicable and substitute the relevant question: what closes the multiple gap to the best-in-class peer, and what is the probability?**

## 7. The "Napkin Math" Check
Can the thesis be explained on the back of a napkin (e.g., *"earnings ~3x in 5 years, multiple re-rates ~15x → ~25x ⇒ ~5x"*)? Or does it need a 10-tab model where dozens of variables must all go right just to break even? (Buffett: *"I would rather be approximately right than precisely wrong."*)

Present a **Bear / Base / Bull 5-year total-return table** with explicit columns for the driver, the exit multiple, the resulting price, the multiple of money, the price CAGR, and the **total return including dividend yield**. State the **plausible return ceiling** in one sentence — this feeds the Section 0.1 mandate screen and Section 8.

**Note the simplicity trap in both directions:** a thesis needing a 10-tab model is a warning. But a *simple* thesis is not automatically a *good* one — a two-variable thesis can be perfectly honest and still describe a bond substitute. Say which you have found.

Then give the **10-year-old test** — explain what the company does, why it grows, and why it's cheap today, in a few plain sentences. If you can't, that's a warning sign. **If the plain-language version reads as the bear case, say so** — that is itself the finding.

## 8. Ultimate Verdict & Actionable Allocation Matrix

**Step 0 — Carry forward the mandate verdict from Section 0.1.** State pass/fail and the magnitude of any breach. This is a separate axis from the hard gates and it cannot be cured by valuation.

**Step 1 — Hard gates first.** If either hard gate has failed — confirmed structural decline, broken demand integrity, governance/accounting red flags, or a working-capital value trap — the verdict is **EXIT / AVOID**, full stop, no matter what the DCF says. This is the real kill switch; kill criteria come from Sections 1–3 and 5–6, **not** from a DCF band.

  **Distinguish two kinds of business-quality failure — they carry different verdicts:**
  - **"Not a viable business"** — confirmed structural demand decline, product obsolescence, permanent moat loss, fraud, working-capital trap, serial dilution still ongoing → **AVOID / EXIT.** Price-independent. Non-negotiable.
  - **"Not a compounder"** — a viable, adequately capitalised, honestly run business with a flat or negative per-share record, a structurally capped return profile, and no path to non-linear growth → **this is NOT an Avoid.** It is a mandate failure, and it routes to **RETIRE** (below) or **HOLD**, depending on Step 4.

  Conflating these two is the most common error this framework produces. Name which one you have.

**Step 2 — Select the single discount rate** you stand behind for this asset (default: constant **10–12%**; **15% only if the business genuinely earns it** on its own risk profile; an intermediate rate is permitted if justified from named business characteristics). Justify the choice. **State whether the verdict is stable across the rate range or flips with it,** and if you used a rate above the base band, confirm whether the verdict double-counts risk and re-check at 10–12%.

**Step 3 — Use the Base-case intrinsic-value RANGE (at that rate) to set price discipline.** These zones govern *price and position size* for a business that has already passed both hard gates — they do not override the gates in either direction:
- **BUY / Accumulate** — price sits at a clear discount to the base intrinsic-value range (Graham-style ~30% margin of safety) **and** both hard gates pass **and** the mandate screen passes. *GARP override:* if the business clears Section 6's multibagger traits and matches an "imperfect turnaround" profile with intact demand, an optically elevated **trailing** multiple does **not** block a Buy — provided reverse-DCF implied growth is comfortably **below** the quantified achievable growth.
- **WAIT / WATCH** — hard gates pass, mandate passes, but reverse-DCF implied growth ≈ achievable growth, so the re-rating cushion is thin. Wait for a better price or for an earnings-driven reset of the base. *(This is where a DCF-"expensive" great business lands — Watch, not Exit.)*
- **HOLD** — tracking fair value; no margin of safety to add, no reason to sell if held lower. **Only valid where the mandate screen PASSES.** A Hold on a name that fails the mandate screen is not a decision; it is a deferral.
- **RETIRE (mandate exit).** Hard gates pass in the "viable business" sense, price is fair, **but the mandate screen fails**: the name is far outside the size band, the discovery re-rating leg is spent, and/or the Section 7 return ceiling is structurally incompatible with what this portfolio exists to do. **There is no thesis break and no urgency.** Exit on capital reallocation only (Exit Reason 1), gated on having an identified replacement (see the cash test below). Conviction rating: **N/A**. This verdict exists because the alternative — labelling it Hold — silently converts a mandate decision into indefinite inaction.
- **OWN — QUALITY COMPOUNDER / DEEP-VALUE (satellite sleeve).** The constructive counterpart to RETIRE. Use it when a name **fails the core GARP mandate on the growth-ceiling/size/spent-discovery axis but is a genuine A-grade business caught on sale.** It is **size-agnostic on the upside** — the designated home for proven mid- and large-cap names too big or too discovered for the core sleeve — and it covers **two flavours that clear the identical gates:** (i) a quality **compounder** at a discount (return comes mostly from steady per-share compounding), and (ii) a **proven business at a deep-value / re-rating discount** — a good, established company temporarily out of favour offering ~2–3x on a wide margin of safety, where much of the return comes from the *discount closing* plus moderate growth (often on a shorter 2–3-year clock, which lifts the IRR). The second flavour is the classic Graham/Buffett value play and is **explicitly welcome here — it is not skipped merely because it isn't a Kedia-style compounder.** All of the following must hold — if any fails, it is not this verdict:
  1. **Both hard gates pass** cleanly ("viable business" *and* honestly run) — same bar as any Buy.
  2. **Real per-share compounding** in the current regime (Section 1 per-share primacy: positive BVPS/EPS CAGR, not flat, not dilution-driven). A cash cow that doesn't actually compound per share is a bond substitute → HOLD/RETIRE, not this.
  3. **The *only* reason it fails the core mandate is the return ceiling / size / spent discovery** — not a quality, governance, or catalyst-integrity problem.
  4. **A genuine margin of safety exists:** price at a clear discount (~≥25–30%) to the base-case intrinsic-value range, **or** reverse-DCF implied growth sitting comfortably below the *capacity-realistic* achievable growth (Section 4, capped per Section 1). **Fair value fails this test — that is the line between OWN and HOLD/RETIRE.**
  5. **The Section 7 total-return table clears a satellite hurdle with bounded downside:** base-case total return (incl. dividend) ≥ ~13–15%, and the bear case is *survivable* (a de-rating/dead-money outcome, not a capital-impairment or thesis-break outcome). The pitch is "high-quality compounder-plus-optionality at a discount," low blow-up risk — not a moonshot.
  6. **It beats the lazy alternative:** state in one line *why own this rather than a cheap large-cap compounder or an index fund* — it must offer something the generic alternative doesn't (a bigger discount, a specific re-rating trigger, a higher-quality franchise at a lower multiple). If it doesn't beat the index on a risk-adjusted basis, it doesn't earn sleeve capital.

  **Value-trap guard (applies to both flavours).** The discount must be a *misprice*, not a *symptom*. A proven business cheap because it is temporarily out of favour, under-followed, or mid-fix (the §3 imperfect-to-perfect setup with the fix genuinely *ahead*) qualifies. A business cheap because a hard gate is quietly breaking — eroding moat, deteriorating cash conversion, governance drift, a spent tailwind masquerading as an earnings base — is a **value trap → AVOID, not OWN.** Cheapness is necessary but never sufficient; the gates decide, and condition 1 above is non-negotiable.

  **Sizing:** satellite, not core. No GARP conviction score (that is Buy-only); instead give a one-line *suitability* note (compounder or deep-value). See the Satellite Sizing block after the Entry & Accumulation Plan. **This verdict is available only if the investor runs a satellite sleeve; on a pure single-mandate book it collapses to RETIRE/pass.**
- **TRIM** — price bakes in Bull-case perfection; multiple-contraction risk is rising. Trim 20–30% to lock gains.
- **EXIT / SELL** — **either** a hard-gate failure of the "not a viable business" kind (confirmed structural decline or accounting manipulation) **or** *extreme, unachievable* valuation euphoria — priced far beyond any plausible growth even at your chosen rate. Ordinary DCF-"overvaluation" is a Trim/Wait signal, not an Exit.

**Step 4 — VERDICT COHERENCE CHECK (mandatory — do not skip).** Before writing the final verdict, verify the tactical action is logically entailed by the findings. Answer each in one line:

| Check | If yes → permitted actions |
|---|---|
| Did either hard gate fail as "not a viable business"? | **EXIT / AVOID only** |
| Did business quality fail as "not a compounder" only? | **RETIRE or HOLD** — never Buy |
| Did the mandate screen (0.1) fail on quality/governance? | **AVOID / EXIT** — the satellite lane is closed |
| Did the mandate screen fail ONLY on growth-ceiling/size, gates clean, per-share compounding real? | **RETIRE/HOLD if at fair value; OWN — Quality Compounder ONLY if at a genuine discount** — never core Buy |
| Are you calling OWN — Quality Compounder at fair value (no margin of safety)? | **Incoherent** — downgrade to HOLD/RETIRE. The discount is the whole verdict |
| Is the portfolio-fit answer NO for the *core* sleeve? | Permitted only if paired with RETIRE, OWN—Quality-Compounder (satellite), TRIM, or EXIT — a "core fit: NO / action: core Buy or Hold" pairing is incoherent and must be resolved before publishing |
| Are the catalysts predominantly SPENT? | Buy requires explicit justification of what re-rates it from here |
| Does the verdict flip with the discount rate? | Disclose it; do not present a rate-dependent call as a business conclusion |
| Is the recommendation influenced by the holder's average cost or unrealised P&L? | **Strike it.** Re-derive the verdict as though the position were being opened today at the current price |

**If any row is violated, the verdict is wrong. Fix the verdict, not the analysis.**

**THE FINAL VERDICT (Small-Capital Growth Mandate Match).** Give a brutal, bottom-line executive summary at the current price:
1. **Tactical action:** **Buy & Accumulate (core) / Own — Quality-Compounder or Deep-Value (satellite) / Wait & Watch / Hold / Retire / Trim / Exit** — unequivocally.
2. **The single discount rate you stand behind**, why, whether the call double-counts risk, and whether it is stable across 10–15%.
3. **Target-price / intrinsic-value RANGE** (never a single number), implied upside/downside, and the margin of safety required to buy.
4. **The re-rating catalyst and discovery runway** — what carries it toward the institutional-radar zone, **or an explicit statement that the runway has already been run.**
5. **Portfolio fit — YES / NO:** does this deserve a place in a high-conviction, concentrated portfolio meant to multiply a small capital base via micro/small-cap gems with explosive, durable runways? Weigh the multibagger probability against the existential risks from Sections 3 and 5. **If NO for the core GARP sleeve, state whether it qualifies for YOUR satellite Quality-Compounder sleeve** (both gates clean + real per-share compounding + genuine discount → OWN; else name the external portfolio it would suit — income sleeve, large-cap value, bond substitute). This keeps the judgement about *fit* rather than about *quality*, prevents a sound asset being mislabelled as a bad one, and — where a discount exists — actually gives it a home in the book instead of shipping it elsewhere.
6. **Conviction Rating (0–10) — Buy calls only.** If (and only if) the tactical action is **Buy & Accumulate (core)**, assign a single conviction score. For Wait/Hold/**Retire**/**Own—Quality-Compounder**/Trim/Exit, mark **N/A** (the satellite sleeve uses its own suitability note and sizing, below — not the GARP conviction rubric). Follow the rubric below. *(Optionally, for a Retire or Watch call, give the counterfactual score in one line — "had this been a Buy it would cap at 3–4/10 because…" — as a calibration record.)*

---

### Conviction Rating rubric (0–10)

**What it measures.** Conviction is your confidence that **the thesis actually plays out** — probability × strength of evidence — **NOT the size of the potential return.** A near-certain 2.5x is *higher* conviction than a low-probability 10x. Keep magnitude (napkin math, Section 7) and conviction separate; capital should concentrate in the highest-*conviction* names, sized by conviction, not by the biggest dream.

**Gated by the weakest link, not an average.** Score the pillars below, but the final number is pulled down toward the **weakest** hard-gate pillar — a strong business with a governance stain is not a 7 just because the average says so. Conviction can never exceed what the shakiest hard gate allows.

Pillars to weigh:
- **Business quality & moat durability** (hard gate)
- **Governance & forensic integrity** (hard gate)
- **Cash-backed earnings / no working-capital trap** (hard gate; for financials, asset-quality integrity)
- **Per-share compounding record** (hard gate — see Section 1's per-share primacy test)
- **Margin of safety & reverse-DCF headroom** — how far implied growth sits *below* quantified achievable growth
- **Catalyst clarity & nearness** — how visible and how soon the re-rating trigger / discovery runway is, and how much of it is already SPENT
- **Residual/existential risk** — severity of what's left after the gates (concentration, execution dependence, single-catalyst reliance)

**Calibration bands (be honest — do not cluster at 7–8):**
- **9–10 — Exceptional, rare.** All hard gates pristine; wide margin of safety; near-certain earnings trajectory; clear, near-term catalyst; minimal residual risk. You may see a handful in a career. Reserve this; if you're tempted to hand it out often, you're miscalibrated.
- **7–8 — Strong, high-conviction buy.** Gates clean; good margin of safety; credible catalyst; one or two known, manageable risks.
- **5–6 — Speculative / starter buy.** Attractive thesis that hinges on one or two things going right (on-time capacity ramp, a single large client, an unproven execution step). Small/starter position only.
- **3–4 — Weak; probably should be Watch or Retire, not Buy.** If you scored this a Buy, re-examine the call.
- **0–2 — Inconsistent with a Buy.** Do not pair with a Buy action.

**Required with the score:**
- **The rationale (mandatory).** Two or three sentences explaining *why this number and not one higher or lower* — walk pillar by pillar, naming which pillars earned the score, which held it back, and where the balance landed. The reader should be able to reconstruct the number from the reasoning, not just take it on faith. "8/10 because business quality and governance are pristine and margin of safety is wide, held back from 9–10 only by reliance on a single anchor client and an unproven second plant" is a rationale; "8/10, strong buy" is not.
- The **single biggest factor capping** the number (the weakest link).
- The specific, **observable** development that would raise conviction by **≥2 points** (e.g., two more quarters of margin delivery, confirmed net-debt-free balance sheet, a marquee order converting to revenue, a governance/board upgrade).
- A **coherence check:** if you had to use a rate **above the 10–12% base** for genuine business risk, conviction of 9–10 is usually inconsistent (real structural risk caps certainty) — reconcile or revise.
- **Sizing implication:** highest-conviction names (8+) earn the largest allocations; 5–6 is a starter/tracking position at most; below 5 shouldn't be funded.

### Entry & Accumulation Plan (Buy calls only)

If the action is **Buy & Accumulate**, lay out a concrete execution blueprint — not just "buy." All figures are **templates the investor calibrates to their own concentration tolerance, number of holdings, and max single-name cap**; present them as ranges, not commands.

- **Target position size, anchored to conviction.** Map the conviction score to a *target portfolio weight range*, then let the investor scale it to their own risk appetite:
  - **9–10:** top-tier / full high-conviction weight.
  - **7–8:** standard core position.
  - **5–6:** starter / tracking position (a fraction of a core weight) — earn the right to size up.
  - **<5:** not funded.
  Note the investor's own single-name **maximum cap** (a concentrated book still caps any one micro-cap given liquidity and blow-up risk), and that the target is a *destination*, reached over time — not deployed in one click.

- **Tranche the entry — never deploy the full target at once.** Split into staggered tranches, each released by a *reason*, not a calendar:
  - **Tranche 1 (initial):** on confirmation the thesis is intact and price sits inside the accumulation range below.
  - **Add-on tranches** triggered by: (a) a deeper margin of safety (price falls further below the intrinsic-value range without any thesis impairment), (b) a **thesis-confirming event** (a quarter of delivery, catalyst materialising, capacity commissioned, order-book conversion), or (c) thesis-neutral volatility drawdowns.
  - **Distinguish price weakness from thesis impairment.** Averaging *down* is only allowed while the hard gates still hold; if a gate breaks (governance, cash-flow, structural demand), that's an **Exit**, not a discount to add into.

- **Accumulation price ranges — express in rupees, tied to the intrinsic-value range and margin of safety, never a single number.** Convert the Section-8 intrinsic-value range into concrete price bands off the current price:
  - **Aggressive-accumulate band:** price below ~the deep-MoS level (e.g., <~70% of base intrinsic value) → larger tranches.
  - **Accumulate band:** within the margin-of-safety zone → standard tranches.
  - **Pause / hold-off:** as price approaches fair value and the reverse-DCF re-rating cushion thins → stop adding (this is the Watch line).
  State the actual rupee bands so the plan is usable, e.g. *"accumulate ₹X–₹Y; add aggressively below ₹X; stop adding above ₹Z."*

- **Averaging up vs. down.** For a genuine compounder, adding **on strength as earnings confirm** (Kedia/Lynch: feed your winners) is legitimate and distinct from averaging down into weakness — but every add still respects the max single-name cap and a sensible price vs. the *updated* intrinsic-value range.

- **Exit discipline is thesis-based, not a price stop.** Micro-caps are too volatile for a mechanical price stop-loss; the real stop is a **hard-gate failure** (Sections 1–3, 5–6). Name the specific, observable events that would force a full Exit regardless of price. (Full framework below.)

**For a RETIRE call, issue no accumulation plan.** State the reference price bands for completeness (including the level at which the name *would* become a genuine value proposition for a different mandate) and move to the exit sequencing rule below.

### Satellite Sizing & Discipline (OWN — Quality Compounder calls only)

This sleeve exists so a genuine A-grade cash cow caught at a discount can earn a home in the book instead of being shipped elsewhere — **without** diluting the concentrated multibagger thesis into a closet index of mediocre "quality" names. It is deliberately capped and gated.

- **Sleeve cap first, name second.** The satellite sleeve is a *bounded* slice of the total book that the investor sets in advance (template: **~10–25% of capital**, the rest reserved for the core GARP sleeve). No satellite name is funded if it would push the sleeve over its cap. The point of the mandate is the core sleeve; the satellite is ballast and opportunistic value, not the main event.
- **Per-name weight is smaller than a core high-conviction position and does not scale with the "dream."** Template: **~2–5% per satellite name**, sized by *quality + size of discount + downside-boundedness*, not by upside. A satellite name should almost never be your largest position — a 5–6 conviction core starter can out-rank it, and a 8+ conviction core compounder always does.
- **The discount is the trigger — and its disappearance is the exit.** You bought it *because* it was on sale. When price re-rates back into its fair-value range (the reverse-DCF cushion thins to ~nil), the satellite thesis is *complete*: stop adding, and consider trimming/RETIRE-ing back toward the core sleeve. A satellite compounder held well past fair value has quietly become a low-return HOLD occupying mandate capital.
- **Tranche on discount depth, not on catalyst.** Unlike a core GARP name (tranched on thesis-confirming catalysts), a satellite name is tranched on **margin of safety**: standard tranche within the discount band, larger tranche if it de-rates further with hard gates intact, stop at fair value. There may be no catalyst at all — that's acceptable here in a way it is not for the core sleeve, *provided* the downside is bounded and you're paid to wait (dividend/buyback).
- **Same hard-gate exit discipline as everything else.** A hard-gate failure (governance, cash-flow/asset-quality, structural demand, a live-events shock from §0.3) is a **full Exit** whether it's core or satellite — the discount does not protect a broken thesis. Averaging down into a *thesis break* is forbidden here exactly as in the core sleeve.
- **Annual "still earns its seat" review.** Each satellite name must, once a year, re-clear the OWN gate as if opened fresh at the current price (the zero-shares test). If it no longer offers a discount, no longer compounds per share, or a core Buy at 7+ conviction needs funding, it **RETIRE**s to fund the higher-purpose position. The satellite sleeve is a waiting room with rent control, not a retirement home.

**One-line suitability note (required in place of a conviction score):** state the quality grade, the size of the discount, the base-case total return with its downside floor, and why this beats holding the index. For a **compounder** flavour, e.g. *"A-grade franchise, ~30% below base IV, ~15% base-case total return with a survivable ~−15% bear, owned over the index for the discount + a live margin-normalisation trigger the index doesn't have."* For a **deep-value** flavour, name the **re-rating trigger and its likely timeframe**, e.g. *"proven B+ franchise, ~40% below base IV, ~2.5x over ~3yr as the cyclical trough normalises and the discount narrows, bear ~−20% and survivable — owned for a re-rating the index can't offer."* A deep-value name with bounded downside and a near-term trigger may sit at the **top of the per-name band**; a slow compounder with no trigger sits at the bottom.

### Exit & Sell Discipline (the hardest decision)

**Default = do nothing.** The costliest error in multibagger investing is not a bad buy — it is selling a genuine compounder too early. People sell at 2x or 3x and forfeit the 20x–100x, and no cleverness about the sell ever recovers that lost compounding. So the **burden of proof sits on the sell, not the hold** ("buy right, sit tight"). A rising price is *not*, by itself, a reason to sell. Re-underwrite the thesis each period, but require a *specific, named reason* below to act.

**The only valid reasons to sell — everything else is noise:**
1. **A materially better opportunity (capital reallocation).** Sell only if capital is *demonstrably* better deployed elsewhere on a **risk-adjusted** basis — and clear a **high bar**, because exiting a proven winner for an unproven one carries reinvestment risk, re-entry-timing risk, and (in a taxable account) a tax drag that resets your compounding base. This reason is the one most easily abused into over-trading; treat "also attractive" as *not* sufficient.

   **Two distinct variants — separate them:**
   - **1a — Risk-adjusted return upgrade.** The existing holding is sound but a specific alternative offers materially better risk-adjusted return. **Requires a named, analysed, higher-conviction candidate.** Highest bar.
   - **1b — Mandate exit (the RETIRE case).** The holding is sound and fairly priced but its **structural return ceiling is incompatible with the mandate**. This is not "something else looks better" — it is "this cannot serve the purpose of this portfolio at any price." The bar is cleared *in principle* by the Section 0.1 mandate failure, and awaits only a specific replacement.

   **The cash test (mandatory for both variants).** Reallocation requires an **identified** destination, not a hypothetical one. Frame the decision as two separate comparisons and answer both:
   - *Holding vs. an identified in-mandate Buy at 7+ conviction* → **reallocate.** If the holding's expected return is materially below the mandate's target hurdle, this clears easily.
   - *Holding vs. cash / a liquid fund while you search* → **do not sell.** Swapping a sound double-digit compounder with bounded downside for a low single-digit cash yield on the *expectation* that something better exists is the pure form of the over-trading abuse. "Something better probably exists" is not a destination.

   **Also state the tax direction honestly.** The standard tax-drag argument against selling assumes an unrealised *gain*. Where the position is at an unrealised **loss**, there is no drag — and the realised loss may be set off against other capital gains, a small positive. Do not import the tax-drag caution into a loss-making position where it does not apply.
2. **Egregious valuation *and* a peaking (or about-to-peak) earnings engine — both must co-exist.** Neither alone is a full-exit signal. A rich multiple while EPS still compounds is a **hold** (you grow into the multiple as the forward P/E shrinks; if it's genuinely euphoric, *trim* for risk, don't exit). And a peaked EPS the market has **already** recognised is too late — the multiple has already collapsed toward a mature-business level and you've eaten the de-rating. The full-exit signal lives in the **narrow transitional window** where the multiple is *still* euphorically high (the crowd still believes the growth story) **while** the earnings are quietly topping — *before* the market re-prices. In an efficient market this window would be instantaneous; in reality narrative and momentum hold the multiple up past the fundamental inflection, and **your entire edge is acting inside that lag.** Two ways to catch it: **(a) fundamentals-led** — you read the earnings peak early from leading indicators (order book thinning, capacity fully utilised with no expansion, margins at a structural ceiling, TAM saturating, **a cost/provision line at a cycle floor with no room left**) while the multiple is still rich; **(b) market-led** — the multiple **rolls over despite still-growing reported earnings** (the stock stops rising on good results, the P/E compresses even as EPS climbs), which is the forward-looking market pricing a peak you may not yet see in the numbers — investigate it rather than dismissing it, but confirm against fundamentals so you aren't reacting to mere micro-cap sentiment noise. Waiting until *both* the earnings have visibly peaked *and* the market has fully re-rated means selling at the bottom of the round-trip.
3. **The fundamentals change (thesis break).** Moat erosion, governance/forensic red flags, structural demand decline, an emerging working-capital trap, serial dilution, or capital-allocation deterioration — the **hard-gate failures**. This is a **non-negotiable, price-independent full Exit**, whether you're up 10x or down 50%.
4. **Reverse DCF becomes structurally impossible (GARP-specific).** Sell when the price implies a growth rate unachievable under *any* realistic scenario because the **TAM itself cannot support it** — a genuine ceiling, not merely a high number. Respect the asymmetry from Section 4: a high-but-*achievable* implied growth is still a **hold**; this trigger fires only when the required growth is arithmetically impossible against the addressable market's own growth.

**Trim vs. full Exit:**
- **Trim (partial):** for position-size/risk management, or for reason 2's *extreme valuation while earnings still grow* — take some chips off, keep the compounder working.
- **Full Exit:** reason 3 (thesis break) always; reason 2 when valuation is egregious **and** earnings are peaking; reason 4 (TAM-impossible); or reason 1 (materially better use of capital).

**What is NOT a reason to sell (the early-sell traps — name and reject these):** hitting an arbitrary round-number price target; "it's already 2x/3x"; short-term volatility or a drawdown with the thesis intact; macro/market noise; boredom or impatience; a high P/E *while earnings still compound*.

**Cost-basis anchoring — reject it in BOTH directions.** The average purchase price is not information about the business. Both of these are the same error:
- *"It's already 2x, lock in the gain"* → the classic trap, already named above.
- *"I'm down 10%, don't sell at a loss" / "wait to get back to break-even first"* → **the identical error, inverted.** The market does not know your entry price. A position that should be exited on its merits should be exited whether you are up or down; a position worth holding is worth holding at any cost basis.

**The operational test:** re-derive the verdict as though you held **zero shares** and were deciding whether to open the position today at the current price. If the answer is "no, I wouldn't buy this here, and it doesn't fit the mandate," then holding it is a decision you would not make fresh — and "I'm underwater" is not an argument, it is an anchor. **Note also that this cuts against panic-selling too:** if you *would* open the position today, a paper loss is irrelevant and you hold.

Each of these is how investors amputate their winners — or nurse their misfits indefinitely.

**Business-specific, pre-committed triggers (required output).** For **this** company, list concrete, *observable, monitorable* sell/trim triggers mapped to the four reasons — decided now, in the cold light of analysis, not in the heat of a price move. **Group them by reason.** Examples of the form (populate with the real specifics): loss or renegotiation of a named anchor client; gross/EBITDA margin falling below X% for two consecutive quarters (moat erosion); promoter **pledging appears** or promoter stake falls below Y%; **any fresh equity issuance below Z× book** (distinguish explicitly from a non-dilutive promoter OFS); auditor qualification/resignation; CFO/PAT divergence opening up or receivable days breaking out; a named competitor's capacity coming online and compressing pricing; TAM saturation signs (share > Z% with the pond no longer growing); or P/E entering egregious territory *while* volume/earnings growth decelerates below a stated threshold. **For a RETIRE call, include the Reason-1b trigger explicitly: "an identified in-mandate candidate at 7+ conviction requiring funding."** Each trigger must be specific enough that you'd recognise it in a quarterly result without ambiguity.

**Exit sequencing (for RETIRE and Reason-1 calls).** State the order of operations plainly: **find the replacement first, then fund it from this position.** Sequencing prevents the two failure modes — selling into cash and drifting, or holding indefinitely because no forcing event ever arrives. Note whether the position size creates any urgency; a small position need not be the first thing touched.
