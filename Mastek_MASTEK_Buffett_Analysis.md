## 1. THE BUSINESS — Understanding the Economic Engine

### Business Model

Mastek Ltd, established in 1982, is a mid-cap Indian IT services company focused on **digital engineering, cloud transformation (especially Oracle Cloud), and sector-specific technology services**. The company serves clients primarily in:

- **Government & Public Sector** (UK NHS, HMRC, and other UK government bodies — historically the largest revenue contributor)
- **Healthcare & Life Sciences**
- **Financial Services**
- **Retail & Manufacturing**

**Geographic Mix:** The UK & Europe contribute the majority of revenue (~60-65%), followed by North America (~25-30%) and India/Rest of World (~10%). This UK government dependence is both a strength (large, sticky contracts) and a risk (single-geography concentration).

### Buffett Simplicity Test

Would Buffett understand this business? **Yes — with a caveat.** At its core, Mastek sells brainpower — it deploys skilled engineers to help organisations modernise their IT systems, move to the cloud, and build digital platforms. The revenue model is straightforward: bill hours or deliver projects for a fee. Buffett famously said: *"I try to invest in businesses that are so wonderful that an idiot can run them. Because sooner or later, one will."* IT services businesses are people-dependent, not idiot-proof — but the best ones (like Mastek) build deep domain expertise and long-term client relationships that create meaningful switching costs.

### Economic Engine Classification

Mastek is a **switching-cost business** with elements of a **specialised service franchise**. Once embedded in a client's IT infrastructure (especially government systems where compliance, security clearances, and domain knowledge create high switching barriers), the cost and risk of switching vendors is substantial. The UK government work, in particular, creates a quasi-annuity stream — these contracts run for years with extensions.

**Recent Strategic Win (April 2026):** Mastek secured a two-year £15 million contract with the UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), with a possible two-year extension — reinforcing the UK government franchise.

## 10. COMPETITIVE POSITION & MOAT ANALYSIS

### Moat Assessment

**Moat Type: Narrow — Switching Costs + Specialisation**
